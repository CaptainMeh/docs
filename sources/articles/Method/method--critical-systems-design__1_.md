# /method/critical-systems-design

---

- This is the primary identity anchor page. Everything else on the site should be traceable back to something on this page.

- "Critical" does not mean safety-critical only. The word means: systems where poor design produces measurable costs. The method applies wherever interface quality has real operational consequences — whether those consequences are safety incidents, revenue loss, stalled decisions, or degraded professional performance.

- The distinction that matters: not regulated vs unregulated. It's systems where design quality has operational consequences vs systems where it doesn't.

- Examples of non-obvious critical systems: logistics platform where dispatchers can't understand system state under load. Financial operations tool where exception handling is ambiguous. Clinical workflow platform where handoff info is structured for ideal case but breaks on the common one.

- Core claim: competitive performance emerges from understanding the terrain of reality in which systems operate — actual workflows, technical constraints, unspoken mental models, systemic dysfunction, limits of what can be known.

- The alternative: design as pattern application — taking established UI conventions, research rituals, best practices and applying them to new contexts. Produces interfaces that look like design was done but lack substance. Flows are generic. Features feel hollow. System fragments under real use.

- Five principles: evidence-aware thinking, multi-perspective synthesis, lateral exploration, tension-driven reasoning, growing systems organically.

- Five phases: Sandbox Experiments, Concept Convergence, Iterative System Building, Organizational Integration, Implementation Partnership.

- Phases are not waterfall stages. They flow into each other. Often return to earlier phases when new information emerges. More like progressive crystallization than linear process.

- What this produces (three categories): products that maintain competitive position, systems with substance, organisations that can compete.

- "More elegant, less nonsensical, capable of evolution, protected from meaninglessness" — this phrase from the source doc is worth preserving somewhere on this page.

- The method requires: evidence-aware thinking, multi-perspective synthesis, lateral exploration, tension-driven reasoning, growing systems organically.

- Not every engagement includes all five phases. Adaptations exist for audit/research-focused work, small tactical projects, large transformation programs.

- Grounded instance of phase compression — IMServ. The clearest portfolio example that the method's shape holds under extreme constraint. A one-week build sprint produced a deliberately disposable thirteen-screen pitch prototype, built for a buyer (an energy supplier evaluating a competitive tender) rather than for end users, with no usability testing. Almost every normal parameter of an engagement was inverted: the audience was a buyer not a user, the artefact was disposable not durable, the timeline was days not months. The method still ran recognisably through its phases — domain learning and option space mapping (Sandbox Experiments), a compressed convergence committing the information hierarchy, UI direction, and scope against an immovable demo date (Concept Convergence), the build of the navigable flow (Iterative System Building) — and then terminated in a genuine Implementation Partnership: after the tender was won, IMServ stood up its own internal design team and Creative Navy supported it over roughly seven months to independent operation. The backbone of the prototype still grew from operational reality (a five-stage meter journey mirroring real settlement practice) rather than from generic dashboard patterns. Use this as the concrete example when stating that adaptations exist for small tactical projects, and as evidence that "independent operation by the client's own team" is the real endpoint even for a compressed engagement. Evidence basis: client-reported (tender win; product manager Peter McFord stated IMServ had a solid foundation to build the product itself). No measured user outcomes — state this when citing.
