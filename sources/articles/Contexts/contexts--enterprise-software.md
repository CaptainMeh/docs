## Evidence to draw on

### Triopsis workforce management SaaS (most measurement-rich enterprise case)
- Workforce management for utilities and road maintenance — schedulers, operations managers, field technicians coordinating thousands of interventions per week
- 47 microtasks mapped across 3 personas before any redesign decisions were made
- 43 user interviews, 21 participants; 3 in-situ observation sessions under real-time pressure (weather incidents, crew shortages, overlapping jobs); 5 stakeholder interviews
- **62% faster job discovery; 83% faster job sequence optimisation; 58% faster weekly planning. All three measured in product analytics from real users in the live system — not usability testing task times, not estimates.**
- **Training: mandatory 1-hour remote session → optional 15-minute video; approximately 90% of users self-onboarding. Client-reported operational data.**
- **Support ticket volume: "how can I" questions fell to approximately 5% of previous volume. Client-reported.**
- **Sales conversions multiplied by four; company began winning clients 4–5x larger than before. Client-reported by CEO.**
- **Tender scores improved by 10–20% attributable to design quality, depending on tender weighting criteria. Client-reported from formal tender evaluation documents.**
- 68-component design system, 200+ documented states, 15 workflow types — built as reasoning documentation
- 2-year Implementation Partnership after delivery — designers present for the full growth phase

### Gexcon CFD simulation software (expert enterprise, user base expansion)
- Computational fluid dynamics simulation for industrial safety engineering — gas dispersion, explosion risk, facility safety validation
- Brief at engagement start: extend product life by 25 years; retain scientific rigour; open access to newer engineers and non-technical roles (risk managers, safety analysts)
- 102 individual tasks documented; 24 user interviews; 23 workplace observations; 9 stakeholder interviews; 12 competitors benchmarked; 45 design variants across 10 key challenges; 37 evaluation sessions
- **Time to first successful simulation: 4 days → 6 hours. Measured by Gexcon in real deployments.**
- **Configuration errors per simulation: 5–8 → 1–2. Measured by Gexcon.**
- **Corrective load per error: 4–6 hours → approximately 20 minutes. Measured by Gexcon.**
- **Active users per team: 1 → 3–4. Client-reported.**
- **Training: 3-day instructor-led events → short webinars and video materials. Operational change.**
- 3-year total engagement (4 weeks research, 6 weeks option space mapping, 7 months execution, 2-year Implementation Partnership)
- Franz Zdravistch, Chief Training Engineer: attributed the team's self-directed domain learning speed as exceptional even by expert standards — direct quote available in case study file, confirms depth of domain immersion

### Polymatica analytics platform (capability democratisation, international expansion)
- GPU-backed OLAP analytics platform — technically superior but required personal training from the founder to use
- Before redesign: **2% of users completed key operations independently without help documentation.** After release 1: 40%. After release 2: **56%. Measured via product analytics.**
- Training model: Roman (founder) delivered personal training for every new customer → users could operate independently post-redesign
- **International expansion to UK, US, Germany became possible** — previously blocked because Roman's English was limited and he spoke no German. Client-reported.
- HSBC and Barclays came on as UK clients post-redesign — client-reported. At their data volumes, the GPU performance advantage (50–100x faster than competitors) became experientially perceptible for the first time.
- 8-month design engagement + 2-year Implementation Partnership; 67 support requests, 2-hour average response time

### Chemical Watch compliance intelligence platform (subscription price and exit)
- Compliance intelligence for chemical regulation professionals — REACH, SVHC, GHS/CLP, TSCA, multi-jurisdictional coverage
- Transformation from news publication to compliance intelligence platform
- **Subscription price tripled following the platform redesign. Client-reported as direct outcome.**
- **24x EBITDA exit multiple one year after launch. Client-reported; the design's role is in the causal chain: design → user adoption of features → justified price increase → revenue → valuation. Frame this chain explicitly, not as "the design caused the exit."**
- Google cited as one of Chemical Watch's enterprise subscribers; a representative described it as the best usability they had seen in a while — reported in a client meeting, not in writing, so cannot be quoted directly
- Engagement: 6 months design, 5 support requests over 6 months post-handover (minimal handover friction)

### Dancerace / Jacko invoice management (conversion rate)
- B2B invoice management and accounts receivable portal — three-party structure (financier, supplier, debtor)
- Greenfield product; 5-month engagement across all five phases
- **Demo-to-paying conversion: 36%. Industry benchmark at time of launch: 15–20%. Dancerace's own expectation was in the benchmark range. Client-reported, measured over 6 months post-launch.**
- The 36% figure is the result of designing for the user's hierarchy of needs (what do I owe, what is my cost, what must I do now) rather than the stakeholder's desire to showcase premium features — this mechanism should be explained, not just the number cited

### Pixelart Fugo digital signage CMS (NPS and revenue)
- Web CMS for managing digital signage content across locations — advertising agencies, hospitality, convention centres
- **NPS: 57% → 89%. Client-measured: survey of all existing users, single measurement before engagement and approximately 2 months after redesigned platform launched.**
- **Revenue doubled in the two years following launch. Client-reported directly. The redesign was part of a broader growth phase that also included rebranding — frame as a contributing factor, not sole cause.**
- 2-year Implementation Partnership structured as monthly developer show-and-tell sessions — confidence support for a development team unfamiliar with the new design system

### IDEXX Animana veterinary practice management (audit scope)
- 11-year-old platform; 35 clinics, 150+ participants, 2 weeks, 3 countries (Netherlands, UK, Germany)
- Central discovery: reception staff and clinical staff have fundamentally incompatible cognitive requirements — receptionists need ambient multitasking, clinicians need focused sequential attention. Standard unified interfaces degrade both.
- 100+ recommendations structured for development ticket translation
- 5-year product vision delivered
- Client-reported 6 months post-engagement: recommendations are well-grounded, some already implemented, remainder planned. Pace reflects internal organisational velocity, not issues with the recommendations.
- Demonstrates Creative Navy's audit capability — the engagement was research and vision, not full design execution

### Tetra / Prism property compliance (mobile adoption, NPS)
- Property compliance and management — field-based property managers (mobile) and office-based directors/portfolio managers (web)
- At engagement start: **12% of users who should have been using the mobile app were doing so.** One year after launch: **64%. Client-measured.**
- **Web NPS: 72% → 85%. Client-measured, approximately 4 months post-launch.**
- Key mobile problem: app downloaded entire portfolio on launch (up to 10-minute load time) — design solution was a property selection flow at launch limiting download to that day's properties. Architecture problem solved through design, not engineering.
- 2-year Implementation Partnership: 12 support tickets total, average 1-hour response time

### Enhesa legal compliance platform (NPS with clean attribution, training burden signal)
- Web-based legal compliance intelligence platform serving regulatory affairs managers, compliance officers, and legal teams. Users navigate dense legislative content across jurisdictions — baseline regulation pages aggregating legislative text, implementation timelines, requirements, and changes. Primary users are domain experts; approximately 20% are not expert users of the platform.
- Engagement covered three platform areas: baseline regulation pages (primary content architecture), dashboard (customisable landing page with multiple data visualisations), and account settings and onboarding.
- Research method: **95 session recordings reviewed; 31 workarounds documented across four categories** (Finding legal texts: 8; Using legal texts: 17; Account settings: 2; Other: 4); **12 error types documented with time-to-redress measurement.** 6 user interviews. The concentration of 17 workarounds in the primary content area was the diagnostic signal: the highest density of compensating behaviour was at the platform's core function.
- Design operated within an existing Enhesa design system — no changes to colours, typography, or existing components. **7 new components added** to address problems the existing system could not solve. Implementations timeline required 5 iteration rounds to find a solution satisfying both client requirements and design system constraints simultaneously.
- Design education delivered as a promised deliverable at every presentation — each review included explicit explanation of user behaviour evidence informing design decisions, enabling stakeholders to evaluate options on evidential rather than aesthetic grounds.
- **NPS: 68% → 84%, measured two months post-launch. Enhesa confirmed no other changes were made to the product between the 68% baseline and the 84% post-launch measurement — clean attribution to the design change specifically. Client-measured across full user base.** The 68% baseline, measured two months before the engagement began, was one of the factors that prompted Enhesa to seek design help.
- **NPS at two years: 87%. Client-measured. Subsequent product changes were made after the 84% measurement — the two-year figure is not attributable to the design work in isolation.**
- **Training burden signal: 45% of pre-redesign users sought training videos; 81% of those said the videos were not helpful. Only 21% of post-redesign users sought training videos.** Enhesa's stated mechanism: the redesigned platform was sufficiently self-explanatory that users no longer needed supplementary help. The 81% unhelpfulness rate confirms the training programme was not compensating for what the interface was failing to provide. Both figures from the same NPS survey. Pre/post comparison is across cohorts, not a longitudinal panel.
- 80% of documented workarounds addressed in the redesign. Error outcomes: 3 error types made structurally impossible, 5 made easier to recover from, 6 expected to reduce in frequency (inferred from design decisions; not independently measured post-deployment).
- One year of Implementation Partnership following design delivery.
- Evidence calibration: NPS figures and training video data client-measured by Enhesa. Clean attribution window confirmed by Enhesa. Post-redesign error outcomes are inferred, not independently measured.
