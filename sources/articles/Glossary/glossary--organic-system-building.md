# /glossary/our-concepts/organic-system-building

---

- Also referred to in source as "growing systems organically."

- Core claim: designs are created as learning experiences that teach what the solution should be, not as solutions themselves.

- Most agencies create designs as solutions. The approach here is different: designs teach what the solution should be. Production designs grow out of this exploration rather than being created from templates.

- Works from both ends simultaneously: micro-level user actions AND system-level coherence.

- Explores laterally: understanding and learning, not just pushing toward a final design.

- Tolerates ambiguity: the space before clarity is where real solutions form.

- Constraint respecting is part of organic building: treat legacy as constraints to work within, not obstacles to overcome.

- Coherence emerges from reality, not from imposed frameworks. The distinction: most methods impose structure on a problem. Organic system building grows structure from what's actually there.

- What this produces: systems where every element has specific value to users, micro-level interactions fit how people actually work, system-level structure reflects the real situation of use.

- Multiple options developed not as "concepts to choose from" but as ways to understand the problem space. Most designs exist to teach, not to be built.

- Why this matters competitively: organic growth prevents fragmentation and preserves meaning under change. As competitors add features that make their products harder to use, systems built through this approach maintain clarity.

- Relationship to sense decay: organic system building is the mechanism that prevents sense decay during the design process itself. Imposed frameworks can produce apparent coherence that breaks down when it meets real use. Coherence grown from observed reality is more durable.

- Relationship to option space mapping: option space mapping is the specific practice; organic system building is the broader principle that governs how the system as a whole develops.

- Grounded example — Assemblio Composer and Smart Viewer (shared components as a two-sided case): Assemblio GmbH and both products may be named — the **Assemblio Composer**, the planning tool used by engineers, and the **Assemblio Smart Viewer**, the shop-floor application used by assembly workers; Assemblio's customers may not be named. The Smart Viewer had been **built from the Composer's components** and looked almost identical to it, while the requirements for the two user groups differ (N36). Shared components are what let the second product exist at all, and what made it wrong. The structural decision that followed — **separating the Smart Viewer from the Composer as its own interface** rather than continuing it as a read-only clone — was the client's: Assemblio arrived at the engagement having already diagnosed that the two user groups needed different products (N54). The decision that followed from it was selective rather than wholesale — not "stop sharing components" but deciding, component by component, which inheritance was economy and which was error. Calibration: credit the separation diagnosis to Assemblio; no time-saving, adoption or commercial figure may be attached, and **Assemblio must never be framed as AI work**.

- Grounded example — Egma Order (four transaction types, three surfaces and an administration area on one chassis): Egma and **Egma Order**, Egma's B2B lens ordering platform, may be named. **The platform is live and nothing about its performance was measured — no post-launch figure of any kind.** Scope covered the web platform, a native mobile application and the administration area, spanning four order types — RX (a prescription order for a named patient, which goes into production), Stock (finished lenses, no production time), Bulk Stock (store-level replenishment) and Services (fitting, tinting and coating applied to a lens Egma did not make) — plus Home, the Order Center, Get Help and Master (G4). One chassis grown to carry all of it. The clearest component-level instance is the table cell (G50): table content on this platform is unusually varied, so **the table cell was built as a single component absorbing header cells, plain text, clickable tags, input fields, image thumbnails, icon buttons, checkboxes and toggles, each with default, hover and selected states** — building the variation into one component rather than designing tables individually is what makes the table variants across the platform cost almost nothing to produce.

- Grounded example — the maritime VTS engagement (a system extracted from a working reference product, then corrected by its own transfer programme): the client is described ONLY as **a European vendor of maritime traffic monitoring, inland navigation and communications software for port authorities, coastal administrations and waterway operators**; **no quotation marks and no reported speech on any page carrying this engagement.** The design system was extracted from a working reference product rather than specified upfront — version 1.0 held **54 components and 198 named states**, in four families (26 general controls with 88 states, 12 operational components with 50, 10 geospatial with 39, and 6 communications with 21), plus eight composition patterns, two themes and two density profiles, with a named state counted once regardless of theme, density, application or repeated instance (W65). The mechanism that corrected it was the transfer programme: **seventy component applications assessed across four products in the two sprints following detailed design — 45 adopted directly, 15 requiring documented extension, 10 explicitly refused; 64.3% direct** (W70). Structure grown from a real product, then disciplined against three more real products rather than imposed on them. Calibration: the 54 components and the 70 assessed applications are different units and must never be added together or merged with any other engagement's inventory; the design system reached production, but **no design-fidelity comparison is recorded — never "shipped as designed"** — and no engineering-efficiency or commercial outcome may be claimed. No engagement dates — sequence and duration only.
