# /glossary/our-concepts/organic-system-building

---

- Also referred to in source as "growing systems organically."

- Core claim: designs are created as learning experiences that teach what the solution should be, not as solutions themselves.

- Most agencies create designs as solutions. The approach here is different: designs teach what the solution should be. Production designs grow out of this exploration rather than being created from templates.

- Works from both ends simultaneously: micro-level user actions AND system-level coherence.

- Explores laterally: understanding and learning, not just pushing toward a final design.

- Tolerates ambiguity: the space before clarity is where real solutions form.

- Constraint respecting is part of organic building: treat legacy as constraints to work within, not obstacles to overcome.

- Coherence emerges from reality, not from imposed frameworks. The distinction: most methods impose structure on a problem. Organic system building grows structure from what's actually there.

- What this produces: systems where every element has specific value to users, micro-level interactions fit how people actually work, system-level structure reflects the real situation of use.

- Multiple options developed not as "concepts to choose from" but as ways to understand the problem space. Most designs exist to teach, not to be built.

- Why this matters competitively: organic growth prevents fragmentation and preserves meaning under change. As competitors add features that make their products harder to use, systems built through this approach maintain clarity.

- Relationship to sense decay: organic system building is the mechanism that prevents sense decay during the design process itself. Imposed frameworks can produce apparent coherence that breaks down when it meets real use. Coherence grown from observed reality is more durable.

- Relationship to option space mapping: option space mapping is the specific practice; organic system building is the broader principle that governs how the system as a whole develops.
