# /glossary/our-concepts/progressive-specification

---

- From source lexicon: "research → requirements → specs → system" — the sequential movement from understanding to specification to system.

- Listed as a strategic concept in the lexicon but not given its own extended treatment in the philosophy/methodology doc. Will need to be inferred and constructed carefully from what's available.

- What can be inferred from context: progressive specification is the principle that design decisions become progressively more specific as the engagement moves through phases. Early phases work at high levels of abstraction. Later phases resolve detail. This is deliberate — premature specificity locks in decisions before the terrain is understood.

- Relationship to sandbox experiments: early phases explore rather than specify. Specificity is earned through exploration, not assumed upfront.

- Relationship to the four-iteration architecture approach: each iteration of structural deliverables produces a more specific version of the architecture. Iteration 1 is broad logic. Iteration 4 is detailed optimisation. Specificity increases as understanding increases.

- Relationship to organic system building: you can't impose specificity on a system that hasn't yet revealed what it needs to be. Progressive specification is how specificity is built without being imposed.

- Contrasted with: front-loaded specification, where the full system is specified at the start of the engagement based on initial understanding. This produces specifications that the engagement's own research then contradicts.
