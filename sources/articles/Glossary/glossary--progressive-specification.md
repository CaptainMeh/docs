# /glossary/our-concepts/progressive-specification

---

- From source lexicon: "research → requirements → specs → system" — the sequential movement from understanding to specification to system.

- Listed as a strategic concept in the lexicon but not given its own extended treatment in the philosophy/methodology doc. Will need to be inferred and constructed carefully from what's available.

- What can be inferred from context: progressive specification is the principle that design decisions become progressively more specific as the engagement moves through phases. Early phases work at high levels of abstraction. Later phases resolve detail. This is deliberate — premature specificity locks in decisions before the terrain is understood.

- Relationship to sandbox experiments: early phases explore rather than specify. Specificity is earned through exploration, not assumed upfront.

- Relationship to the four-iteration architecture approach: each iteration of structural deliverables produces a more specific version of the architecture. Iteration 1 is broad logic. Iteration 4 is detailed optimisation. Specificity increases as understanding increases.

- Relationship to organic system building: you can't impose specificity on a system that hasn't yet revealed what it needs to be. Progressive specification is how specificity is built without being imposed.

- Contrasted with: front-loaded specification, where the full system is specified at the start of the engagement based on initial understanding. This produces specifications that the engagement's own research then contradicts.

- Note: this concept needs more source material than is currently available to write a full page. The lexicon entry establishes it as a term but doesn't provide the depth of the other concepts. May need input from the client to flesh out with specifics.

## Evidence to draw on — corpus update

The page was a stub with no named engagements; the corpus now supplies well-evidenced instances, several claimed strongly by their own change sets. **Use no corpus-wide superlative for any single one of them** — more than one addendum claims the lead and the ranking is unreconciled; present each instance on its own substance.

### AIT (a specification that changed during the design block)
- AIT may be named; nothing else from the engagement may.
- A27 — AIT's controller structure specification ran to roughly **900 nodes across eleven levels of hierarchy**, in German, and **changed during the design block**: in one revision a single operating-mode setting became four, one per heating and mixing circuit. Specificity arriving as understanding arrived, not before it.

### The MDU (the specification revised because of the design reviews, with the before and after both in hand)
- **The MDU** may be named; the manufacturer is not. **Quotes printed here are cleared, attributed to role.**
- M62 — **the specification changed because of the design reviews, and its own change log records what the design process produced**: the revision issued after the reviews added the four draught inputs and the offset derivation that the two-offsets finding required (M56 — a mounting offset set once behind the PIN, and per-voyage draught readings that do not sit behind it, because *"you have bending and twisting and torsion and stuff going on in real life"*), added pitch period (M40 — the original specification carried roll period only), added ship breadth, added event logging and data logging, and promoted 48-hour data logging from a cost-driven "nice to have" into the main specification. The movement from understanding to specification is not just observed here but documented by the client, with the before and after both in hand.

### Egma (states resolved into layers, a catalogue resolved into a sequence)
- Egma and **Egma Order** may be named. **The platform is live and nothing about its performance was measured.**
- G49 — **eleven production steps resolved into three layers of disclosure.** Egma's revision document set out the problem and proposed the mechanism — four headline states, the current sub-status on hover, the full breakdown on click; the delivered design keeps the disclosure mechanism and changes the headline set to **three groups — Order Processed, In Production, Delivery — spanning eleven production steps**, with the Orders area split into drafts, in progress and complete. **The disclosure model was proposed by the client and resolved by Creative Navy — write the division of authorship, not a monologue.**
- G48 — approximately **7,000 lens types made tractable by three moves in sequence**: repeat-a-past-order offered first, since most stores reorder the same few types; then filtering by **eight criteria** — brand, index, sphere, cylinder, sub-technology, coating, sun protection and diameter; then a quantities step in which the interface auto-calculates the percentage each lens type represents of the total, so the optometrist can judge the distribution on the spot.

### KISPE Space (a change-control rule across the artefact chain)
- The client is **KISPE Space**, a UK space systems engineering company; nothing else may be named. **No quotation marks and no reported speech on any page carrying KISPE.** The product was built; **Creative Navy did not support the build and has not compared the built interface against the delivered designs** — never "shipped as designed".
- K98 — **a change-control rule was specified and applies across artefacts**, each document built on and constrained by the one before it: if a technical fact or an observed behaviour contradicts a direction, its status and reasoning are updated and every linked journey, behaviour rule and exception screen is revised in the same change. Modifying the visual prototype while leaving the specification inconsistent is defined as a documentation defect, and a superseded decision gets a new record rather than a silent overwrite.

### Schone Airspace Group (a specification that grew in entitlement, not only in detail)
- **Schone Airspace Group** is the canonical name. **No quotation marks and no reported speech.** The product was built and is in test, not deployed.
- D17 — **four native integration gates specified** — terrain reference agreement; command request and observed result semantics; warning severity and lifecycle mapping; recorded session and media identity — each carrying a contract, a verification sequence, the evidence that would constitute a pass, **and the failure disposition: what the interface must do if the source cannot demonstrate the condition.**
- D23 — **a handover protocol distinguishing two classes of change** — changes requiring UX approval (target selection, command lifecycle wording, last-known status semantics, warning acknowledgement, readiness completion, estimate revision, historical provenance, persistence of the live shell) and changes requiring engineering review (protocol event meanings, aircraft permission model, fleet size, payload commands, stream state, terrain datum, cache schema, recording keys) — plus a specified implementation sequence: build the shared command shell first, establish immutable aircraft and request identities, then connect per-aircraft timestamps and alert ownership.
- D51 — **all four gates were subsequently closed**: terrain and route elevations demonstrated on a confirmed common vertical reference; aircraft-scoped command request, acknowledgement, rejection and observed-result events demonstrated against the specified transaction model; alert owner, severity, acknowledgement and clearing events demonstrated from the source; and flight session, aircraft, media and trajectory identifiers demonstrated to form a traceable association. **The distinctive movement: a specification that named its own unresolved conditions, stated what the interface must do while each remained open, assigned each to an engineering function, and then had all four closed. The concept is usually illustrated by specifications that grow in detail; this one grew in entitlement.** The gates were closed by the client's engineering functions — the claim is not that Creative Navy verified a protocol, but that the design named, in advance and in testable terms, exactly which of its own behaviours it was not yet entitled to assert, and those conditions were then demonstrated.

### Raymarine (close to two hundred observable acceptance checks, and eight gates with owners)
- **Raymarine** is the canonical name; **Ray53, Ray63 and Ray73** may be named; **the new radio's name may not be used in any form.** **No quotation marks and no reported speech.** The radio was built and released; **no design-fidelity comparison is recorded — never "shipped as designed".**
- R33 — **acceptance registers across the specification set**: eighteen tests on the physical control contract; twenty on the detailed voice operating flows; sixteen on the calling specification; eighteen on the AIS information hierarchy; eighteen on the event priority matrix; twenty-three on the emergency specification; twenty-four verification scenarios in the exception catalogue; twenty-four on the radio and chartplotter responsibility model; ten UI acceptance criteria at handover; and twenty-eight UI specification checks. **Each is written as an observable condition testable against a prototype, a hardware mockup or a firmware implementation, not as a subjective preference.**
- R35 — **eight named engineering and compliance decision gates recorded at handover, each with a dependency and a named owner**: physical inputs; radio controls; the calling workflow; safety wording, timing and cancellation by market; position data; AIS freshness; chartplotter integration; and revised wording to be re-tested with occasional operators. **The concept is a design stating in testable terms what it is not yet entitled to assert, and then naming who can settle it — which is exactly what the eight gates are.**

### The handheld C2 engagement (the recursive ambiguity method)
- The client is described ONLY as **a European manufacturer of uncrewed systems, including one-way effectors, and the tactical command-and-control software that operates them**; never "loitering munition"; **no quotation marks and no reported speech.** The handheld interface was built; nothing further is said about its status.
- Pair with H8, the condition that produced the method, stated as such: **six constraints stated openly in the delivery plan before work began** — the target hardware had not yet been settled; requirements were shifting and that was understood to be the nature of the client's situation rather than a problem to be fixed before design could start; the client had a strong preference against a large development effort but would fund one if justified; the client was not certain the multi-vehicle problem was solvable by design at all; design conventions including conflicts between NATO and non-NATO standards had to be observed; and the scale of the system exceeded the size of the project.
- H9 — **the recursive ambiguity method**: Creative Navy authored the requirement set first and submitted it for review and enrichment — not to settle the requirements but to establish where ambiguity sat. Each area of unknown then received one of three treatments: the client sought clarification; the designs catered for a range of speculative possibilities, so that design became the vehicle by which options were disseminated inside the client organisation; or the area was deprioritised. **The triage recurred through the project, and the fitness of the method itself was reviewed every two weeks alongside it.**
- H16 — the artefact that carried it: **a requirements table co-authored screen by screen**, with content, actions and priority, and two facing columns — Creative Navy's comments and open questions, and the client's answers. **This work was led and co-authored with the client's design team, not delivered to it.**

### The maritime VTS engagement (requirements to decisions to contracts to acceptance)
- The client is described ONLY as **a European vendor of maritime traffic monitoring, inland navigation and communications software for port authorities, coastal administrations and waterway operators**; **no quotation marks and no reported speech.**
- The chain: W35 — **twenty-eight traceable interaction requirements**, each linked to session evidence, an issue cluster and a decision. W49 — **fourteen approved decisions against eighteen explicitly rejected alternatives**, every consequential decision with a named alternative on the record and the reason it lost. W67 — **each reusable contract carries a minimum specification**: purpose and eligibility **including explicit non-use cases**; information model with source of truth, identifiers, timestamps, unknown values and permissions; behaviour with triggers, transitions, keyboard focus, interruption and recovery; visual portrayal including accessible text, responsive density and night mode; and quality evidence with accepted scenarios, reviewer and remaining exceptions. W92 — **final issue reconciliation**: of the 36 unique issues, 30 were mitigated in the design specifications, 4 assigned to engineering follow-up and 2 deferred by client decision — with the status language defined: **mitigated means addressed in design specifications and evaluated where in scope, and does not mean implemented in every product, proven in production, certified, or cleared from formal safety review. The four safety-review candidates remained open to the client's safety authority regardless of how well they tested.**

## What this page must not claim

- No engagement dates anywhere — sequence and duration only.
- Raymarine: never the new radio's name; never "shipped as designed"; no quotation marks and no reported speech; the acceptance registers are specifications of observable conditions, not completed validations, and no regulatory or conformity credential may be attached — radio protocol behaviour, channel plans, distress procedure and final conformity remained Raymarine's throughout.
- Schone Airspace Group: no quotation marks and no reported speech; the closed gates are an engineering result the design specified, not a Creative Navy test result; built and in test, not deployed; no flight-safety or airworthiness credential.
- KISPE: no quotation marks and no reported speech; the verification suites, regression checks and acceptance scenarios in its artefact chain are **specifications of tests, explicitly recorded as not executed** — never let "verification" or "acceptance" read as completed testing.
- Handheld C2: no quotation marks and no reported speech; "was built" and nothing more; the co-authorship construction (led and co-authored) applies to every deliverable named here.
- Maritime VTS: locked descriptor only; no quotation marks and no reported speech; the register's counting discipline is arithmetic — 36 unique issues is a constant, and none of the counts above may be added together; never "shipped as designed".
- The specifications and registers describe the work; never imply they produced a measured operational result.
- **Superlatives: more than one change set claims this page's best instance (Schone's gates, the MDU's client-documented revision, the handheld C2 method, Raymarine's registers). Do not crown one — the ranking is an open question for the engagement lead.**
