# `/capabilities/design-for-abnormal-and-degraded-scenarios`

Cross-reference the supervising-autonomous-systems page in one sentence: a lost link, stale telemetry and an unknown command outcome are degraded modes of supervision.

---

## Domain vocabulary

- Abnormal conditions / degraded mode / fault state — the conditions that deviate from the intended operating state; in high-consequence contexts, primary design targets, not edge cases
- Edge case as primary design target — the framing shift that distinguishes this capability from standard interface design
- Fault recovery / recovery path — the interface behaviour that supports users in understanding a fault state and returning to a known good condition
- Alarm hierarchy / priority tiering — the organised structure of alerts by urgency and consequence; without hierarchy, everything looks equally important and nothing is acted on
- Sensor fault / calibration drift / data integrity failure — specific fault types in embedded systems; each requires explicit interface behaviour
- Foreseeable misuse — IEC 62366-1 concept; faults and misuses that are foreseeable must be identified and addressed in the design; not all use-related hazards are deliberate
- Hazard-related use scenario — the IEC 62366-1 framework for identifying situations where interface failure produces patient harm; the design must address each identified scenario
- Graceful degradation — the system continues to function at reduced capability rather than failing completely or presenting an unintelligible state
- Redundant cues — multiple independent signals for the same critical state; essential when any single signal may fail under operating conditions (colour under variable theatre lighting; touch under vibration)
- Silent failure — a fault that does not surface to the user; the most dangerous failure mode in embedded systems, because users continue as if the system is functioning correctly
- Sensor cadence — the update interval of a sensor or data source; when multiple sources update at different rates, presenting them simultaneously can create a display that reads as unstable or contradictory

## Evidence to draw on

### Elsner Elektronik / Cala Touch KNX (consumer embedded — sensor faults as routine conditions)
- The smart home environment regularly produces conditions that the interface must handle without alarming ordinary users: sensors in fault state, calibration drift from temperature or humidity changes, delayed sensor readings during firmware update cycles
- These were identified as design targets before any interaction design began — through analysis of how KNX sensors behave in real installations, not from user complaints about specific faults
- Dual-priority alert system: two visual and auditory notification levels (general alerts vs. critical notifications requiring immediate attention). The priority hierarchy was designed to prevent alert fatigue from low-priority sensor notifications, while ensuring critical conditions (complete sensor failure, safety-relevant state changes) still surface
- Animation timing aligned with firmware update intervals — a specific engineering constraint: visual state changes must never drift out of sync with actual thermal values during the firmware's processing cycle. This is a silent failure prevention measure.
- Sensor fault display deliberately calibrated: informative without alarming for ordinary users who have no technical background and no ability to fix the fault; actionable for the systems engineer who can.

### Torqeedo maritime HMI (sensor cadence mismatch — the central design problem)
- Multiple sensor systems updating at different rates: propulsion motors, battery banks (40–200 kWh), generators, and conversion units each have different telemetry frequencies. Presenting them simultaneously without a synchronisation architecture creates a display that reads as unstable and contradictory to a captain managing the vessel.
- The resolution: a grid structure that synchronises the different cadences into a unified display rhythm — captains perceive one coherent system, not three competing data streams. This is the abnormal-condition-prevention measure built into the nominal design.
- Night operations designed explicitly: tested during sea trials through early morning conditions; the relief crews experience when information remains stable during active vessel manoeuvring was a specific finding that shaped stability and predictability requirements
- Storm conditions observed during sea trials — the interface was not just tested under controlled conditions but encountered actual heavy weather

### Cox Marine cluster displays (multi-engine fault scenario testing)
- Scenario testing during Concept Convergence deliberately explored a multi-engine fault scenario — a situation where one or more engines in a six-engine configuration develop fault conditions simultaneously
- The test revealed layouts that made fault presence visible but didn't direct attention to the priority engine. Multiple designs that had passed nominal-state evaluation failed this test because they distributed attention evenly rather than directing it to what mattered most.
- Resolution: dedicated fault-summary area surfacing the highest-priority condition across all engine tiles, plus alarm state highlighting per tile. The structure communicates: something is wrong, and this is where.
- Military night vision mode: an absolute constraint — the display must be operable under night vision goggles, which require specific luminance and contrast ranges. Any design solution that violated this constraint was eliminated regardless of its nominal-state performance.

### Kardion MCS Controller (regulated alarm architecture under IEC 62366-1)
- The alarm architecture was not a design preference — it was governed by IEC 62366-1 requirements for alarm management, derived from the identified use-related hazards
- Requirements included: priority tiering (alarms ranked by clinical urgency); visual differentiation between alarm priority levels (not just colour — IEC 62366-1 requires colour not be the sole differentiator); mute behaviour (muted alarm must remain visible; the system must not allow a muted alarm to be forgotten); alarm state visibility during normal operation (the absence of alarm must be as visible as its presence)
- Layout stability as an abnormal-scenario design requirement: the design standard (no element shifts position across any view transition) exists specifically because surgeons build spatial memory during procedures and depend on that memory when the clinical situation demands rapid response. Any element that shifts position during a view transition — even to accommodate new information — breaks that spatial memory.
- **FDA approval as the process outcome: the alarm architecture contributed to the design passing FDA evaluation as submitted.** Not a specific alarm-design claim but confirmation that the overall process, including alarm design, satisfied the regulatory review.

### deSoutter Medical / Zethon (recognition under divided attention as abnormal standard)
- The operating theatre is the abnormal condition — not a situation where a device occasionally encounters challenging conditions but the device's normal environment, which is inherently abnormal relative to controlled testing conditions
- Variable theatre lighting: a surgical theatre's lighting changes during a procedure as overhead lights are repositioned, surgical headlights are adjusted, and the overhead field illumination varies. Colour as a primary state indicator fails under these conditions.
- Resolution: redundant non-colour cues (spatial position + icon form + colour). Every critical state communicated through three independent channels, so the failure of any single channel under theatre conditions does not remove the signal.
- Divided attention: surgeons' primary attention is on the surgical field; device interaction occurs through brief glances. The design must support recognition — reading the state of the device in a fraction of a second — rather than recall (remembering what a state indicator means) or interpretation (working out what a visual display is showing).
- 8 surgeons in structured review sessions confirmed state verification reduced to brief glance recognition. **Surgeon-reported from design review sessions, not post-deployment measurement.**

### Gexcon CFD simulation (deferred fault — configuration errors with invisible consequences)
- Abnormal scenarios in expert industrial software have a specific character: the error is not immediately visible. A misconfigured simulation runs to completion and produces outputs that look valid. Those outputs feed into a safety assessment. The error surfaces only when the assessment is challenged, re-run, or — in the worst case — during an incident investigation.
- This is a silent failure of a specific type: not a system failure that the interface should signal, but a user error that the interface failed to prevent.
- Before redesign: 5–8 configuration errors per simulation, each requiring 4–6 hours of corrective work. The safety assessment downstream of multiple error-correction cycles carries structural uncertainty.
- The design response was specific: interaction architecture that makes required values visible during scenario setup, surfaces warnings for incomplete or contradictory input, and specifies the system's behaviour when configuration errors are detected — rather than presenting errors as after-the-fact notifications.
- **Configuration errors: 5–8 → 1–2. Measured. Corrective load: 4–6 hours → approximately 20 minutes. Measured.**

### Beissbarth automotive calibration (sequential procedure interruption by fault state)
- Calibration procedures are sequential — each step depends on the previous one, and the technician must understand the current step's state before proceeding
- Fault conditions in a calibration sequence: borderline tolerance values (measurement within range but below confidence threshold), measurement failures (sensor unable to read), and equipment errors (device not communicating correctly) must each be communicated distinctly because each requires a different corrective action from the technician
- The design challenge: fault states must be communicated unambiguously to technicians who are moving around the vehicle, reading the display from 2–3 metres, under variable lighting, with tools in hand. The fault state cannot require stopping, approaching the display, and reading carefully — by the time the technician does this, the calibration sequence may have timed out.
- State communication standard: unambiguous state across all three device types, including fault states, under the full range of operating conditions.

### Squaremind dermatology scanning device (physical process deviation as abnormal condition)

- The abnormal condition in patient-operated scanning is structurally distinct from all other types in this set. In Elsner, Torqeedo, Cox, Kardion, deSoutter, Gexcon, and Beissbarth, the abnormal condition is a system or data fault — sensor failure, misconfiguration, alarm state, degraded hardware. In Squaremind, the abnormal condition is a user-state deviation: the patient moves incorrectly, loses their position in the sequence, or fails to follow a transition instruction. The system itself is functioning correctly; the deviation is human and physical.
- This requires a different design response from fault communication. The interface cannot simply surface a state indicator; it must guide the patient from their current (incorrect) physical state back to the required one — in real time, without a clinician present, for a patient who may be anxious, elderly, or unfamiliar with the device.
- The design response was the Correct layer of the Inform–Prevent–Correct framework: for every step in the scan flow where a patient could deviate, the design specified what the interface must communicate when the deviation occurs, how to guide the patient back to the correct state, and what the system must do after a successful recovery to re-engage the guidance cycle.
- Abnormal condition identification preceded design: the Inform–Prevent–Correct framework required identifying, at every step, which physical deviations could occur, what their consequences would be (scan interrupted, robot arm stops, session ends), and what the recovery path must be. This is the same identification-before-design-decisions principle used in the IEC 62366-1 hazard analysis — applied to a sequential physical process rather than a safety-critical device state.
- **Pre-redesign: 14-patient test by Squaremind produced 0 recoveries from deviation events. Client-reported background.**
- **Post-redesign: 12 patients deviated during the 29-user ecological test; all 12 recovered. Recovery times 2–4 minutes. Creative Navy-measured, ecological protocol.**
- **IEC 62366-1 scope: Creative Navy's role was formative evaluation. Summative validation and regulatory submission are Squaremind's responsibility.**

### The MDU ship motion monitor (the sensor that feeds the interface stops reporting)
- The product may be named — the MDU; the manufacturer is not named.
- The watchdog and no-MDU alarms: the interface has to say something useful when the sensor feeding it stops reporting, and the designed message tells the reader to restart the unit and contact support.
- **Carry the open question with it (M30): Creative Navy's open question in the requirements table was what the user can actually *do* about that screen, and it was never answered in the available material. The question is documented; the answer is not — that is the honest state of that part of the design.**
- Pair with M41: an alarm bound to the rolling 20-minute maximum and minimum rather than the live value is a deliberate choice about which abnormal states are worth surfacing and for how long — an alarm means the vessel was outside its threshold at some point within the last twenty minutes.

### KISPE Space common space operating picture (degradation of information availability — specified, not executed)
- KISPE Space, a UK space systems engineering company. The product is a common space operating picture — a doctrinal term of art, not a trade name. No speech from this engagement is quoted or reported; positions are attributed to the role and the document they came from.
- **This is a class the page's equipment-fault entries do not contain: degradation of information availability rather than of equipment.** A source that cannot be reached, an asset that is restricted, a record that has gone stale, a local change that has not been sent.
- **Lead with the argument: a single global offline banner cannot describe four independent lifecycles.** Source outage, cached content, access restriction and local unsent action each have their own lifecycle, and the deliberately adversarial scenario fixture is the evidence (K42): at the scenario moment the imagery feed is down, the newest image will not render, the highest-resolution image covers only part of the area, one image is restricted, the positioning fix is stale while the communications reading is current, and a predicted overflight has not happened yet — every one a trap the interface has to avoid walking into. **The fixture is a designed test condition, never data, and never represents a real location, operation, satellite or advisory (K41).**
- **40 documented edge cases and degraded states**, each specifying the input condition, the visible response, the recovery path, the misinterpretation it prevents, and a verification that would expose a dangerous misunderstanding (K13).
- **Five principles governing degraded states** (K39): unknown is not adverse — an unreachable provider is not proof that a satellite service has failed; an age label is not a confidence score — observation, receipt and validity have distinct clocks; a catalogue item is not an openable image — metadata, access checks, preview delivery and full asset retrieval fail separately; local is not shared — queued actions remain local until a supported confirmation arrives; a saved snapshot is not a live view.
- Status is seven independent dimensions, not one scale (K55), with the rejected alternative named every time: one green-to-red health score, which would make identical colours carry conflicting meanings across domains.
- The designed behaviours: three availability dimensions exposed independently — device connection, provider feed reachability, locally cached content (K76); cached content keeps its original update time (K77); locally saved is not shared, and reconnection does not complete a synchronisation — an optimistic *shared* confirmation on reconnect was the rejected alternative, comforting and potentially untrue (K78); ambiguous synchronisation outcomes stay ambiguous, with no claim of success or definite failure when the server may have accepted the change (K79); concurrent edits are never silently merged (K80); a partial offline map cache shows its downloaded coverage and its gaps so blank tiles are not read as real geographic absence (K81); losing the connection never clears the confirmed area (K82).
- **The tense is the rule here (K99): five verification suites, eight regression checks and nine acceptance scenarios were written as executable checks — and each is explicitly recorded as not yet executed. This catalogue was specified against a synthetic fixture and was never executed. Never let "verification", "acceptance" or "regression" read as completed testing, and never let the industrial engagement's field observation of a running system lend weight to this specification.**

### Schone Airspace Group Mission Control (degradation of the control channel — operator-evaluated)
- The client is named Schone Airspace Group; "Mission Control" is lower-case descriptive use, never a version number. No speech from this engagement is quoted or reported.
- **Schone adds a second new class: degradation of the control channel** — the path by which an instruction travels becoming unreliable while the aircraft at the far end may be entirely healthy, and the interface having to represent an instruction whose fate is genuinely unknown.
- **Lead with D87: an elapsed response window produces an explicit unknown state that keeps the target, the action and the request identifier, and triggers no automatic retry** — because an unreviewed retry of a consequential flight command is its own hazard. The accepted cost was named and is the sharpest self-criticism in the package: unknown is less reassuring than a binary outcome, but it is accurate and it supports a deliberate subsequent check.
- Control link condition and telemetry freshness are independent fields because they fail independently (D88): a degraded link and a stale sample are different facts with different display semantics, and a value that has stopped updating keeps its last value alongside its age rather than being relabelled as current or hidden. The accepted cost: two indicators take more room than one badge, in exchange for the operator not over-reading a simplified status.
- Eligibility is withheld rather than assumed when the evidence has aged (D89): where link condition and sample freshness cannot establish eligibility, the action shows availability as unknown rather than as ready. The accepted cost was named: the design sacrifices apparent immediate availability in uncertain conditions in order to preserve the accuracy of command affordances.
- **The evidential asymmetry runs in Schone's favour and must not migrate: these behaviours were evaluated against fixed scripted criteria — first candidates 14 pass, 18 fail, 4 pending (D42), the integrated retest 32 pass, 0 fail, 4 pending (D44) — by Creative Navy and then by five working remote pilots. Those are design-conformance results, not task completion rates, error rates or anything measured in flight. The product was built and is in test, not deployed. Never let Schone's evaluation lend weight to KISPE's specification.**

### Raymarine next-generation fixed-mount VHF (across both classes — the enumeration, not a new category)
- The client is named Raymarine; the existing Ray53, Ray63 and Ray73 line may be named; the new radio's name may not — write "the new radio" or "the next-generation fixed-mount VHF". No speech from this engagement is quoted or reported.
- **Raymarine does not get a new class, and the restraint is the point: it sits across information-availability and control-channel degradation rather than forming a fourth category.**
- **Its contribution is the enumeration, and it is substantial** (R133): forty-three exception records organised against **eight state axes that vary independently** — voice radio state, display context, calling transaction, AIS data quality, positioning, safety incident, interruption, chartplotter link — plus twenty-four verification scenarios. **The governing sentence leads the entry: a current screen is not a system state.** The interface is a state interpreter, not the source of truth for radio outcomes.
- The transferable rule is R134: **a failure is attributed to the subsystem that produced it.** A lost chartplotter link is reported as a loss of chart integration and never as a radio fault; an AIS receiver failure never implies an empty sea; a missing registered identity is distinguished from a receiver failure. A per-feature dependency map records which functions survive which failure.
- **Observe the evidential asymmetry, and say which is which: four of the degraded conditions were put to operators on a bench and measured** — the interruption case (resumed unassisted: seven of eight; median resumption 12.60 seconds across all eight attempts, 12.00 seconds across the seven unassisted recoveries — both denominators travel together, R81), the man-overboard comprehension case (activated correctly: eight of eight; understood it had not transmitted an external alert: six of eight, R83), the empty-versus-unavailable AIS case (seven of eight distinguished an unavailable receiver from a genuinely empty traffic picture, R85), and the chartplotter disconnection case (eight of eight retained local voice and digital calling, R86) — **the other thirty-odd were specified and not executed.**
- **These were bench prototype sessions. Raymarine's own brief named wet hands and gloves, glare and night sailing, vibration, ambient noise and divided attention, and none of them was tested (R90).**

### The handheld C2 engagement (interference as an expected operating condition — an argument about intent)
- The client is **a European manufacturer of uncrewed systems, including one-way effectors, and the tactical command-and-control software that operates them**. The engagement covered interface structure, task behaviour, information priority and the clarity of operator control; it did not extend to autonomy, control algorithms, autonomous safety behaviour, the datalink, the effector, targeting logic, or any certification or weapons-review activity. **Creative Navy designed the surface through which a human supervises the system; it did not design the system.** No speech is quoted or reported. The deliverables were work Creative Navy **led and co-authored** with the client's design team.
- **H17 leads, and it is a requirement rather than a design choice, authored by Creative Navy and stated as a system requirement**: the system shall treat electromagnetic interference as an expected operating condition rather than an exceptional failure, because contested electromagnetic conditions are core to the intended environment.
- What follows from it sits in the control set rather than in the display: **a signal-recovery manoeuvre occupying one of four physical push buttons** instead of a return-to-home command (H88) — at the client's suggestion, a manoeuvre in which the vehicle climbs and loiters in order to reacquire the link, rather than a command to come back — and a command-state model in which rejection and timeout are ordinary values rather than errors (H75): requested, sending, awaiting acknowledgement, accepted, rejected, timed out, resulting state confirmed, with the instruction to use only states the technical system can actually determine.
- **This is distinct from Schone's degraded-link material, and the difference must be named.** Schone's argument is epistemic — link condition and information age are different facts, and a value that has stopped arriving keeps its age. **This engagement's argument is about intent**: the interface was specified for an environment where the link is expected to be interfered with, and a quarter of its physical commitment surface is given over to recovering from it.

### Maritime traffic software portfolio (source degradation with a geographic consequence)
- The client is **a European vendor of maritime traffic monitoring, inland navigation and communications software for port authorities, coastal administrations and waterway operators**. No speech from this engagement is quoted or reported.
- **This engagement adds something none of the above has: source degradation with a geographic consequence.** An equipment fault is not only a fault but a statement about **which part of the picture can still be trusted**, and the design requirement was to link the failing source to the affected layers and sector rather than reporting it in a technical list (W43, W47).
- **W43's other half belongs here too**: unavailable, degraded, stale, unknown and permission-restricted are five states, not one, and the legacy interface collapsed them. Time of last observation sat behind secondary inspection; in a degraded state a retained target could keep the same visual treatment as an updating one, so the display did not reliably tell the operator whether a marker represented a current observation or a last known position. An equipment view with no state data could read as an equipment view with no faults.
- The handover failure compounds it (W47): unresolved work was spread across incident, alert, source-health and workspace views, and an operator had to assemble an account of the active situation by cross-checking separate lists.
- **Observe the evidential asymmetry, as the Raymarine entry does: the interrupted-feed and retained-position conditions were put to operators and measured; the remainder were specified and not executed.** The validation fixtures — 48 simulated targets with three traffic alerts, one equipment fault and two advisories; two nearby radar plots beside an unresolved AIS association; seven open handover items — are designed test conditions, not observations, and must be described as such wherever they are described at all.

## What this page must not claim

- **Three classes, separate sections, no blending in a sentence** — and never let one engagement's evidence lend weight to another's: the industrial field observations to KISPE's specification, Schone's operator-adjudicated evaluation to KISPE's unexecuted catalogue, or either to Raymarine's partly evaluated set.
- KISPE: no completed-testing reading of the verification vocabulary (K99); no research, validation, usability or comprehension claim (K26); no shipped-as-designed or design-fidelity claim — the product was built, and Creative Navy did not support the build and has not compared the built interface against the delivered designs.
- Schone: the pass/fail figures are design-conformance results, never outcomes; no flight-safety, airworthiness, certification or regulatory credential; the product is in test, not deployed; nothing was measured in flight; the scenario fixture is synthetic.
- Raymarine: no field, hardware, lighting, glove, vibration, wet-surface or acoustic claim (R90); no type-approval, certification or conformity credential; the proprietary terms *performance in reality* and *competitive vector* are forbidden on any page carrying Raymarine; the radio was built and released — never "shipped as designed".
- Handheld C2: no safety, certification, weapons-review or regulatory credential; no claim the design prevents accidental activation or operator error; status is "was built", with no explanation of the silence; no quotation or reported speech.
- Maritime VTS: a safety flag denotes a need for domain scrutiny by the client's safety authority — a design solution is not a hazard closeout; no claim about live radar, AIS, lock machinery or vessel traffic behaviour (W100); fixtures are fixtures.
- None of these engagements may be drawn into the AI cluster: these products have no model, perform no inference and make no recommendations. Their unknowns are properties of links, protocols, sensors and clocks — not model uncertainty.
