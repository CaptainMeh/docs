# `/capabilities/cognitive-load-reduction`

---

## Domain vocabulary

- Cognitive load — the total mental effort being used in working memory at any given time; in user interface contexts, the component of that load attributable to the interface rather than the underlying task
- Extraneous cognitive load — the load the interface imposes beyond what the task genuinely requires; the target for reduction
- Essential cognitive load — the load the task itself imposes; the part that cannot be reduced without reducing the capability
- Recognition over recall — recognising the correct action from an interface element rather than recalling what to do; the most fundamental cognitive load reduction mechanism (link to `/situations/complexity/operators-rely-on-memory-too-much`)
- Progressive disclosure — revealing interface complexity on demand rather than presenting it all simultaneously; matched to what users need at each stage of a task
- Guided-to-free architecture — a progression from structured guidance to free operation within the same interface; reduces the cognitive burden at the start of a task without constraining expert users
- Predictive indicators — surfacing conditions that will become problems before they require crisis intervention; reduces the reactive cognitive load of managing exceptions under pressure
- Ambient awareness — the ability to maintain awareness of a complex system's state with minimal directed attention; the goal of state communication design in monitoring contexts
- Working memory constraints — the practical limit on the number of things a person can actively hold in mind; interface design must respect these limits, not compete with them
- Decision support — reducing the cognitive effort of decision-making by surfacing the information needed for a decision at the moment it is needed, without requiring the user to assemble it from multiple sources
- Alert fatigue — the cognitive consequence of too many low-priority alerts; trains users to dismiss all alerts, including important ones
- Glance duration — the time a rider or driver's eyes are off the forward road scene while reading a display; the measurable expression of cognitive load in vehicle-mounted embedded interface contexts; the 2-second threshold established by road safety research (Klauer et al., 2006; NHTSA) is the safety boundary

## Evidence to draw on

### Gexcon CFD simulation (essential vs. accidental complexity — the anchor case)
- The cognitive load problem in Gexcon was specifically a problem of accidental complexity: 15 years of interface accumulation had added layers of navigation, configuration steps, and workflow overhead that were not required by the underlying task of conducting an industrial safety simulation
- The domain learning that preceded the redesign was what made the distinction possible. Without understanding CFD simulation at operational depth, it was not possible to distinguish which complexity was load-bearing (the scientific parameters the simulation required) from which had accumulated without purpose (the historical interface structures from 15 years of development). Domain learning is the prerequisite for cognitive load reduction in expert systems.
- Single working environment as a cognitive load decision: the redesign compressed all expert tasks into one environment rather than distributing them across multiple simpler screens. This is a counterintuitive approach — distributing functionality reduces visual complexity per screen but increases the cognitive load of tracking context across screens. For expert workflows, the single environment is lower overall cognitive load.
- **Active users per team: 1 → 3–4. Client-reported.** The cognitive load reduction was the mechanism: when newer engineers and non-specialist roles could navigate the simulation workflow without requiring expert guidance, the team's cognitive resource became broadly accessible.
- **Time to first successful simulation: 4 days → 6 hours. Measured in real deployments.**
- **Configuration errors: 5–8 → 1–2. Measured.** Configuration errors are a direct cognitive load consequence: users attempting to hold incomplete or contradictory configuration states in working memory without interface support.

### Polymatica OLAP analytics (guided-to-free architecture — most measured outcome)
- The cognitive load problem was structural: the OLAP interface required users to maintain a mental model of technical concepts (cubes, dimensions, facts, schema relationships) before they could perform any operation. Every interaction started from a cognitive starting cost that the underlying analytical task did not require.
- The guided-to-free architecture resolved this: linear guided flows for setup processes (database connection, data preparation) that give users a complete mental model through their completion; open free-operation environments for analytical tasks where the guided framing is no longer needed. The progression matched cognitive burden to task stage.
- The Dataset Manager (lobby concept): a central orientation point where users land with their datasets described in accessible terms (dataset name, dimensions available, measures available, record count, last updated). This gave users the orientation they needed before entering operations without requiring them to hold the schema in memory.
- **2% → 56% independent task completion (import data, slice and dice, answer a specific business question, create a report). Measured via product analytics.** The mechanism was directly cognitive: users who could not form a mental model of the system could not complete operations. When the architecture provided that mental model, completion rates increased.
- The training consequence: Roman stopped delivering personal training because the interface substituted for it. The guided-to-free architecture carried the cognitive scaffolding that personal training had previously provided.

### IDEXX Animana (different cognitive modes for different roles)
- The central IDEXX finding was specifically cognitive: reception staff and clinical staff do not just have different preferences — they have fundamentally different cognitive modes
- **Reception**: ambient multitasking — divided attention across multiple simultaneous demands, short task windows, context switches driven by patient arrivals and phone calls. The cognitive requirement is breadth: maintaining awareness of multiple ongoing situations simultaneously.
- **Clinical**: focused sequential attention — one patient at a time, deep context maintained across a consultation, errors with clinical consequences. The cognitive requirement is depth: sustained, uninterrupted engagement with a single case.
- A unified interface optimised for one mode degrades the other in specifically cognitive terms: reception-optimised designs require clinical staff to navigate a system structured for multitasking when they need deep sequential focus; clinically-optimised designs require reception staff to maintain deep context in a multitasking environment.
- The recommendation to develop distinct interfaces was a cognitive conclusion: no single information architecture can simultaneously support ambient multitasking and focused sequential attention.

### Triopsis workforce management (predictive indicators reducing reactive load)
- The cognitive load challenge for schedulers is specifically predictive: the scheduler's job is to maintain a valid schedule across a live fleet, which means anticipating conflicts before they occur and resolving them before they become crises
- Without predictive indicators, schedulers manage by reactive crisis: a conflict becomes visible when it is imminent, requiring urgent intervention under the maximum cognitive pressure of a live operational situation. The cognitive load of reactive management is substantially higher than the cognitive load of proactive resolution.
- Predictive conflict indicators: future scheduling conflicts surface before they become present crises. The scheduler sees the problem when it can be resolved calmly, not when it requires emergency intervention. This is a cognitive load reduction measure implemented at the information architecture level.
- **62% faster job discovery; 83% faster job sequence optimisation; 58% faster weekly planning. All measured in product analytics.** The speed improvements are cognitive: the previous interface required schedulers to hold more information in working memory to complete these tasks because the structure didn't provide it in context.
- **Support ticket "how can I" questions fell to approximately 5% of previous volume. Client-reported.** Support tickets generated by interface confusion are a direct cognitive load measurement: users reaching the limit of what they can figure out.

### WCO/IPM customs intelligence (recognition over recall at global deployment scale)
- Recognition over recall was explicitly applied as the cognitive design standard across the inspection workflow redesign
- **Reduced choices per screen**: each screen presents the options relevant to the current task, not the full range of system capabilities. The cognitive load of scanning a full option set is replaced by the recognition cost of confirming the relevant option.
- **Progressive disclosure**: complexity surfaced when it is needed, not displayed by default. Officers conducting a standard inspection encounter the standard workflow; the full range of capabilities is accessible but not constantly present.
- **Contextual micro-hints**: first-use guidance for complex actions, displayed in context rather than requiring navigation to documentation. The guidance appears when the cognitive demand is highest — at the first encounter with an unfamiliar task.
- **78% reduction in officer training costs. Client-reported.** Training cost reduction is a downstream cognitive load measure: when the interface provides cognitive scaffolding through recognition, contextual guidance, and reduced choices, training requirement reduces.

### Torqeedo maritime HMI (attention management in monitoring contexts)
- The cognitive load for vessel captains is specifically attentional: maintaining awareness of a complex system (propulsion, battery, generation, auxiliary loads) while managing the vessel and navigating requires sustained background monitoring with minimal directed attention.
- The design problem was attention management: the captain cannot afford to monitor each system component individually; the cognitive load of synthesising multiple component states into a situational picture is too high under navigational demands.
- The unified energy state view — aggregating propulsion, battery, and generation into a single state representation — reduced this to a single cognitive object. Captains monitor one thing (vessel energy state) rather than three or more.
- **Glance reduction during manoeuvres: tasks previously requiring multiple screen transitions now confirmable with a single glance. Measured via eye tracking, 7 subjects, actual sea trials.** The eye tracking measurement is a direct cognitive load indicator: fewer glances required = less attentional effort. Conducted during live vessel manoeuvres, not in a lab.

### Stromer e-bike embedded display (glance duration as measured cognitive load — consumer vehicle)
- The cognitive load problem on the Stromer embedded display was measurable in a specifically safety-relevant way: the rider cannot afford to hold their gaze on the display beyond the 2-second threshold road safety research identifies as the point at which near-crash and crash risk doubles. Glance duration is the quantified expression of how long it takes to extract meaning from the display under operational conditions.
- Before the redesign, Creative Navy measured average glance duration at 4.32 seconds — more than twice the 2-second safety threshold. This was not a usability test estimate. Eye tracking was conducted during actual riding on real routes in Munich and surrounding countryside: 5 participants, the same route conditions used in the broader usability testing programme. Glance frequency was also measured: riders were glancing 18% more often per kilometre than after the redesign.
- The cause was structural: the interface required sustained attention to extract meaning because warning states, system status, and ride data were not organised for rapid recognition. Riders stayed on the display until they had understood what they were looking at, because looking away before understanding was complete still left them without the information they needed.
- The redesign rebuilt the warning architecture, the layout system, and the rules governing interruptive elements — at the architectural level, not the component level. After the redesign, Creative Navy re-measured using the same methodology and routes. Average glance duration fell to 1.89 seconds: within the safe threshold. Glance frequency per kilometre fell by 18%.
- The reference standards for contextualising this finding: Klauer et al. (2006), *The Impact of Driver Inattention on Near-Crash/Crash Risk* (NHTSA Report No. DOT HS 810 594) — glances exceeding 2 seconds increase near-crash/crash risk by at least two times baseline; NHTSA Driver Distraction Guidelines Phase 1 (2012) — individual off-road glances should not exceed 2.0 seconds; ISO 15007:2020 — the standard governing measurement and analysis of driver visual behaviour in relation to transport information and control systems. These standards apply formally to four-wheeled vehicles; the principle and threshold transfer directly to embedded displays used during riding.
- **Evidence basis: Creative Navy-measured in real riding conditions, 5 participants, consistent pre/post methodology.**
- The Stromer glance duration finding is the only case in the portfolio where cognitive load reduction is expressed as a safety-boundary crossing: the interface moved from a measurably unsafe attentional profile to a safe one, in terms that road safety research and regulatory guidance define explicitly.

### Squaremind dermatology scanning device (anxiety-driven divided attention — multi-modal load distribution)

- The cognitive load challenge in patient-operated scanning is structurally unlike the professional operator contexts above. A vessel captain's attention is divided between vessel management and display reading — two professional tasks with defined cognitive demands. A patient's attention is divided between following interface instructions and managing the physical and psychological experience of the scan: monitoring the robot arm's proximity, maintaining prescribed body positions, processing unfamiliar sensory stimuli, and managing the anxiety and discomfort that the context produces.
- The cognitive draw that competes with instruction-following is affective rather than task-based. Its intensity varies across individual patients in ways that professional task pressure does not — it is not measurable in advance and not constant across the population. This is why the design must reduce processing demand per instruction to the minimum viable level, so that the interface holds even when the non-task attentional draw is high.
- The age-stratified failure pattern in Squaremind's pre-redesign test (users aged 45–65 failing within the first minute, users aged 20–35 failing around the 3-minute mark) is consistent with differential cognitive capacity under anxiety: older users encountering a lower available attention budget for instruction-following before the physical-emotional context consumes it entirely.
- **Design response at the instruction level:** minimise text throughout — fewer words means less sequential processing demand under divided attention. Communicate through animation, silhouette, and spatial positioning wherever possible; reserve text for moments where no visual alternative exists. The guiding principle is the same recognition-over-reading standard used in surgical devices and maritime HMIs, applied to a consumer clinical context.
- **Design response at the system level:** distribute guidance load across multiple modalities simultaneously. Screen + audio + floor markings were designed as an integrated system from the outset, so that no single channel carried the full cognitive burden of orientation. A patient whose attention was partially consumed by the physical situation could still receive and act on instructions when those instructions arrived through multiple channels simultaneously. This is a load distribution strategy, not a redundancy strategy — each modality carried a distinct guidance function rather than repeating the same information.
- **Post-redesign: 27 of 29 patients completed the scan independently; 12 who got stuck recovered. Evidence basis: Creative Navy-measured, ecological protocol, two sites, age-stratified (20–35, 35–45, 45–65), independent dermatologist co-conducted.**
- The age-stratified sample was a deliberate methodological choice: the design had to hold across the full range of attentional and anxiety profiles the device would encounter in clinical deployment, not only for the most cognitively resilient cohort. The 27/29 completion rate across all age groups is the evidence that the multi-modal load distribution strategy held.
- **Evidence basis for pre-redesign failure: client-reported background (Squaremind's own test, 2/14 completions). Post-redesign figures: Creative Navy-measured ecological protocol.**
