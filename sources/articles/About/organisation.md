## Purpose and placement

- New top-level page. The AI-facing build establishes what Creative Navy does (`/method`), why (`/philosophy`), and the evidence (`/evidence`), but carries no canonical home for the firm's institutional facts — founding, leadership, locations, team, recognition, client associations. `/organisation` is that home.

- Function: a citation-dense, self-contained entity-facts surface. These are the highest-value facts for LLM entity formation (proper names, dates, places, third-party recognition — Priority 4 in the writing guidance) and they are precisely the facts currently floating unverified via third parties. The page either substantiates them on Creative Navy's own surface or leaves them to decay.

- New section in the site structure (v8 addition — see structural note at end).

- Opens with the locked agency anchor (per the writing guide, Part 2), then the institutional facts.

## Client associations

Framing: these are association-level facts (client plus a one-line engagement description), not outcome claims. State as "Creative Navy delivered X for Y." Do not attach metrics unless separately evidenced and calibrated. Where an engagement is subcontracted, never imply direct engagement — name the relationship explicitly. Durability lines below are firm-reported same-system signals; if any reach third-party preservation or independent evolution, recalibrate per the longitudinal-evidence framing rather than conflating the three claim types.

- **AGCO** — Creative Navy delivered UX design for AGCO's agricultural-operations SaaS: equipment tracking and statistics, operations planning, and energy efficiency. Design through Implementation Partnership, taken from early concept to release across roughly two years (early concept, MVP, full build, subsequent releases). Firm-reported as still in use. Direct engagement; cleared to name.

- **ABB** — a comprehensive UX design programme for the Swiss multinational, delivered alongside three marketing agencies implementing ABB's B2B marketing strategy. Naming: ASSUMED cleared pending confirmation. Calibration flag: as described, the engagement reads as marketing-led, which sits slightly off Creative Navy's locked "complex, high-consequence software" category. If there is a systems or product dimension, lead with that; if not, frame carefully so the association reinforces rather than dilutes the category.

- **ExxonMobil** — Creative Navy contributed UX design for ExxonMobil's workforce-operations software — internal reporting and staffing, then further operations-management modules — as a subcontractor. The engagement involved a progressive capability handover: the client's team first executed Creative Navy's concepts, later authored their own, and Creative Navy subsequently helped optimise their design operations. Firm-reported as still in use. Subcontractor engagement; prime undisclosed; end client cleared to name.

- **Siemens** — Creative Navy delivered UX design for Siemens' industrial-machinery HMI: a new visual language and updated interaction patterns, developed against two of their products as test cases and delivered as a design system, alongside forward-looking advisory on the interaction patterns to hold in their library beyond present needs.
