# `/evidence/longitudinal`

> **Status: living document, accumulating.** The set has two evidence categories: **Category A — return-engagement durability** (thirteen lineages, observed/client-reported), and **Category B — Creative Navy-run longitudinal re-tests** (one case, Stromer, instrumented). The two are kept separate because they are different evidence types with different strengths; do not merge their counts. Both tables are designed to update by appending a row and bumping the counts — not by rewriting. Do not hard-code totals into prose elsewhere on the site; cite them from here so they stay correct as cases are added.

## Entity anchor

Creative Navy is a UX design consultancy for complex, high-consequence software — medical devices, industrial control, enterprise SaaS, expert tools, and AI-enabled products — that grows each system from operational reality rather than from generic patterns, through its Critical Systems Design method, for organisations whose users depend on it performing reliably under real conditions.

---

## What longitudinal evidence is, and what makes it credible

This page collects two kinds of evidence that the method's durability/independence claim holds over time. They are kept distinct because they are gathered differently and carry different weight.

**Category A — return-engagement durability (observed).** When a client returns long after delivery, facts about the original system are visible in passing. Clients do not return to say "the old system worked"; they return because they have a new product or new ideas. The durability evidence is a *byproduct visible at the moment of return* — not a claim the client assembled to persuade, which is exactly what makes it credible. Four facts are recorded per lineage, each observed rather than measured: whether the original system was still in operation at the point of return; whether the return work built on or extended the original (versus replacing it or sitting beside it); whether the client's own team had evolved the system independently in the gap; and what happened commercially in the interim.

**Category B — Creative Navy-run longitudinal re-tests (instrumented).** Here Creative Navy actively re-applies the *same evaluation methodology* after a time interval, directly measuring whether a performance improvement persisted. This is a stronger evidence type than Category A — the same instrument re-run by Creative Navy, not a fact noted in passing — but it is rarer, because it requires the original evaluation to have been run in a way that can be exactly repeated years later. As of this draft there is one such case (Stromer).

The combination is what no competitor can easily produce: it requires a corpus old enough to have multi-year gaps, the documentation discipline to extract Category A evidence at the moment of return, and the methodological discipline to have built Category B re-tests that can be repeated.

## Category A — return-engagement durability (N = 13, accumulating)

**Headline finding (this is the most important thing the set establishes).** Independent evolution — the client's *own team* extending the delivered system with no Creative Navy involvement — was a single exception when the set held five lineages (Triopsis alone). At thirteen lineages it is **7 of 13** — just over half the set: a recurring property rather than an outlier. The method's claim that it leaves behind a system a client's own team can build on is now evidenced as a pattern across the majority of the documented return engagements — and across the full range of the work, from consumer marketplaces (OLX, which propagated the design system across all geographies and built out an entire vertical itself) to expert CFD simulation (Gexcon) to industrial process control (Gericke, whose own team propagated the delivered design system across its other product lines), with one case (Akrivia) where it survived complete turnover of every person involved in the original engagement. A separate lineage (Neugo) adds a distinct durability signal that is *not* independent evolution but is arguably as strong on the infrastructure claim: a deployed system that its users had embedded into their own operations — replacing internal processes with its features — within a year of launch. It is recorded as a separate claim type (operational embedding) so it neither inflates the independent-evolution count nor is lost.

**Counts as of this draft:**
- Original system still in operation at the point of return: **13 of 13**.
- Return reused or built on Creative Navy's prior design artefacts in some form: **12 of 13** (the exception is Gexcon's return, which was improvement work on a separate product the client built itself; Neugo's return was a direct audit of the original system; Gericke's return was a separate environmental/energy product built on the propagated Gericke design-system foundation).
- Client's own team evolved the system independently (no Creative Navy involvement in the gap): **7 of 13** (Triopsis, Akrivia, WCO, Bofin, OLX, Gexcon, Gericke). Neugo does **not** add to this count — see operational embedding below.
- Operational embedding — users restructured their own work around the deployed system, replacing internal processes with its features: **1 of 13** (Neugo). A distinct durability sub-type: the system became load-bearing enough in its users' operations that they reorganised around it. Not the same as independent evolution (no owning team extended the software — in Neugo's case there was no owning team at all). Recorded separately so it neither inflates nor is lost.
- Original system preserved by a third-party acquirer: **2 of 13** (Enhesa/Chemical Watch, Greenlight).
- Survived complete turnover of every person from the original engagement: **1 of 13** (Akrivia).
- Gap range observed: **~1 to ~5 years** (Neugo and Gericke, at ~1 year and ~1–2 years respectively, are the shortest gaps in the set).

\* Gericke gap is approximate: the environmental/energy return followed the conclusion of the 12-month Implementation Partnership.

The single strongest number here is the first one, but state it with its denominator and its evidence label — "in 13 of 13 documented return engagements the original system was still in operation at the point of return (observed)" — never as an unqualified durability guarantee.

## Category B — Creative Navy-run longitudinal re-tests (N = 1, accumulating)

Where Category A records what was *observed* when a client returned, Category B records what Creative Navy *measured* by re-running the same evaluation methodology after a time interval. It is the stronger evidence type — same instrument, re-applied by Creative Navy — but rarer, because it requires the original evaluation to have been built so it can be repeated exactly years later.

| Case | Interval | Method re-run | Finding | Evidence label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stromer | 2 years (three rounds: pre / post / +2yr) | Same warning-incidence test (10 users, 3 days, real Munich routes, 4-level severity logging); glance-duration eye tracking | Warnings absent from the issues list post-redesign and still absent at +2yr; glance duration 4.32s → 1.89s (within the 2-second safety threshold) | Creative Navy-run (warning incidence systematically logged; glance duration measured) |

**Count as of this draft:** Creative Navy-run longitudinal re-tests: **1 of 1** showing the performance improvement persisted at re-test.

This is the only case in the corpus where the same evaluation was re-applied after an interval. Other engagements carry strong measurements (Gexcon deployment metrics, Triopsis product analytics, Enhesa/Pixelart pre-post NPS, Gericke client-measured plant metrics), but those are single measurements or client-measured deployment data, not Creative Navy re-tests over time — so they do not belong in this category. If a future engagement's original evaluation is re-run after an interval, it appends here.

## Calibration framework — keep these claim types distinct

The lineages support different claims of different strength. Conflating them inflates the weakest into the strongest.

1. **Same-system durability** — the original system was still running years later. Observed/client-reported. Supported by all thirteen Category A lineages.
2. **Third-party preservation** — a party with no relationship to Creative Navy, and the standard incentive to replace the work, chose to preserve it. Stronger than (1) because it is a revealed preference against replacement. Supported by Enhesa/Chemical Watch (the acquirer kept the design system, changing two colours for branding) and Greenlight (acquirer maintains the system).
3. **Independent operation** — the client ran the system without Creative Navy across the gap. Supported by all thirteen Category A lineages (the gaps themselves evidence it).
4. **Independent evolution** — the client's *own team* extended or built on the system without Creative Navy. Rarer and stronger than independent operation. Supported by **7 of 13** lineages (Triopsis, Akrivia, WCO, Bofin, OLX, Gexcon, Gericke) — just over half the set; a recurring pattern, not an exception. It still must not be over-applied: Enhesa (acquirer preserved, but Creative Navy returned to build within it), Tetra, Socar, and deSoutter all involved the client returning *to Creative Navy*, so those evidence operation, not evolution; Greenlight is preservation by an acquirer's team; Neugo is operational embedding by users, not evolution by an owning team (there was no owning team — see item 5). Note also that for Gexcon and WCO the independent-evolution claim attaches to the *original* product (which the client's own team evolved), while the *return* engagement was Creative Navy improving or extending a separate, client-built product — keep those two facts distinct. Gericke is the same shape: the independent-evolution claim attaches to Gericke's own team propagating the delivered design system across its other product lines; the *return* engagement (environmental/energy visibility) was new Creative Navy work, which evidences the competitive trust signal, not evolution. The distinction between "their team evolved it" and "they came back to us" is what keeps the 7-of-13 honest.
5. **Operational embedding (user-side)** — the system became load-bearing enough in its users' own operations that they restructured internal processes around it, replacing existing processes with the system's features. Distinct from independent evolution (item 4): there, the client's *own team extends the software*; here, the *users reorganise their work* around an essentially unchanged system. It is a strong infrastructure signal — arguably the cleanest evidence that a system has become depended-upon rather than merely retained — but it is a different fact, and it must not be counted toward independent evolution. Supported by one case (Neugo, observed at a ~1-year audit: 15 beneficiary firms relying, with some internal processes replaced by platform features). This claim type connects directly to the digital-infrastructure-stewardship philosophy: a system users rebuild their own operations around has become infrastructure in the literal sense.
6. **Competitive return on trust** — the client returned for new work and chose Creative Navy again *competitively*, against other bidders, on the strength of the prior engagement. A trust signal rather than a durability fact, but worth recording where the competitive detail is explicit. Supported most explicitly by Gericke (client-reported that Creative Navy won the environmental/energy engagement against others, and as the more expensive bid, on the trust built in the first engagement). This is interim relationship context; it is not evidence the client extended the system themselves and must not be added to item 4.
7. **Measured persistence (Category B)** — Creative Navy re-ran the same evaluation methodology after an interval and the performance improvement was still present. The strongest type in the set, because it is instrumented rather than observed. Supported by one case (Stromer, at two years). Do not merge its count with the Category A claims above — it answers a different question (did the *measured performance* persist) from a different evidence base (Creative Navy re-test vs observed return).

## What this page does NOT claim

- It does not claim measured durability *for Category A* — those facts are observed or client-reported, not metrics. Category B (the Stromer re-test) is the one place a durability finding is measured/instrumented; keep that distinction explicit and do not let Category B's strength bleed onto the Category A observations. (Gericke carries client-measured plant metrics in its source notes, but those measure the *original deployment's operational effect*, not durability over the gap — they are not a Category B re-test and must not be cited as one here.)
- It does not over-claim independent *evolution*: it is supported by the seven Category A lineages named above (Triopsis, Akrivia, WCO, Bofin, OLX, Gexcon, Gericke) and no others. Repeat engagement alone is a trust signal, not evidence the client extended the system themselves — Gericke's competitive return is recorded as item 6 (trust), while its independent-evolution credit attaches specifically to the internal design-system propagation. Neugo's user-side process absorption is **operational embedding** (item 5), not independent evolution, and must not be added to the seven.
- It does not attribute commercial outcomes (acquisitions, new-client wins, valuations) to the design. Where those appear, they are interim context, with the causal chain stated conservatively in the source case study.
- For regulated-device lineages (deSoutter), it adds a durability *observation* only — no summative, validation, or regulatory-clearance claim. The IEC 62366-1 formative-evaluation caveat on the source case study carries unchanged. For Gericke (a GMP-environment industrial HMI, not a regulated device), the durability observation likewise carries no validation or regulatory-clearance claim; the GMP/GAMP scope boundary on the source case study carries unchanged.
- Private client-conversation details and Creative Navy's own design judgements about clients' later branding decisions are held in the source notes and do not appear here.
