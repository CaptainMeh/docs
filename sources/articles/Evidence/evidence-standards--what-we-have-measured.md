# `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-we-have-measured`

---

## Opening — entity anchor

Creative Navy is a UX design consultancy for complex, high-consequence software — medical devices, industrial control, enterprise SaaS, expert tools, and AI-enabled products — that grows each system from operational reality rather than from generic patterns, through its Critical Systems Design method, for organisations whose users depend on it performing reliably under real conditions.

This page documents the outcomes from Creative Navy engagements that carry the strongest evidential basis: outcomes that were directly measured, using defined instruments, under real or controlled conditions, with a stated methodology.

---

## What "measured" means on this site

A measured outcome is one where a specific quantity was recorded using a defined instrument or method, under conditions that are stated and relevant to real use. This category excludes outcomes reported by the client without a disclosed measurement methodology, outcomes that were observed directionally without quantification, and outcomes inferred from the structure of a system rather than tested against it.

The distinction matters because measured outcomes can be interrogated in a way that client-reported or inferred outcomes cannot. They carry a baseline, a method, a condition, and a figure. They can be challenged on their own terms.

Within the measured category, outcomes still differ in how cleanly they attribute the change to the design. The strongest form is a randomised controlled comparison: a treatment measured against a simultaneous holdout drawn from the same population, which isolates the design change from everything else happening over the measurement period. The eToro entry is the portfolio's example. Most other measured outcomes here rest on a before/after comparison and earn attribution confidence through other means — a confirmed no-other-changes window (Enhesa, Gericke), metrics the client already owned and operated (UNICEF, Gericke), or measurement in real operating conditions (Gexcon, Torqeedo, Stromer). The standard for appearing on this page is that the quantity was measured by a defined method; the degree of causal isolation is stated per entry.

Creative Navy applies this standard to its own portfolio. The examples below are the claims from its case history that satisfy it. Where an outcome does not satisfy it — even if it is commercially significant — it appears on a different page in this section.

**Note on mixed-basis before/after comparisons:** some entries below involve a before figure that is client-reported and an after figure that is Creative Navy-measured. This structure is legitimate — measuring the post-redesign state under defined conditions is meaningful even when the pre-redesign baseline was not measured under the same conditions — but it requires accurate labelling of both figures. The CDR Foodlab and Squaremind entries are the current examples of this pattern in the portfolio; the distinction is stated explicitly in each.

## Measured outcomes from Creative Navy engagements

### Gexcon — industrial CFD simulation software

Four metrics were measured by Gexcon across real deployment locations after the Critical Systems Design engagement. These are operational measurements, not usability test results.

- Time to first successful simulation: reduced from 4 days to 6 hours — a 93% reduction.
- Configuration errors per simulation: reduced from 5–8 to 1–2.
- Corrective load per error: reduced from 4–6 hours to approximately 20 minutes.
- Active users per team: increased from 1 to 3–4.

Evidential basis: measured by Gexcon across deployment locations. These are operational data from the live system, not controlled test conditions. The measurement method was Gexcon's own deployment tracking.

### Triopsis — workforce management SaaS

Three productivity metrics were captured through product analytics from real users operating the live system in professional field conditions.

- Job discovery: 62% faster.
- Job sequence optimisation: 83% faster.
- Weekly planning: 58% faster.

Evidential basis: product analytics from the live system, real users under real operational conditions. Not a controlled experiment — these figures come from actual usage data in deployed production software.

### Torqeedo — maritime HMI

Two outcomes were measured under real maritime conditions across sea trial testing with professional captains.

- Energy state identification: 50% faster than the legacy interface. Measured in a controlled experiment with 24 subjects. Conditions were real sea trial conditions, not laboratory simulations.
- Glance reduction during manoeuvres: measured using eye tracking in actual sea trials (7 subjects). Precise glance counts are not published, but the direction is measured, not inferred.

Evidential basis: controlled experiment (24 subjects) for the speed finding; eye-tracking measurement in live sea trials (7 subjects) for the glance finding. The conditions — vibration, night operations, vessel movement, cold water glare — are the conditions of professional maritime work, not reproductions of it.

### Typewise — mobile keyboard

Creative Navy ran a controlled experiment with 60 users towards the end of the engagement, measuring against the iOS native keyboard as baseline.

- Error rate: halved compared to iOS native keyboard.
- Typing speed: increased from 38 WPM to 47 WPM.

Evidential basis: directly measured in controlled testing during the engagement. 60-user sample. iOS native keyboard as explicit baseline. Not client-reported. The testing setup was designed to create realistic pressure conditions so that qualitative feedback could be gathered alongside the measurements.

### eToro — multi-asset social trading platform

After Creative Navy redesigned eToro's landing, buy, and explore surfaces, eToro measured the impact through a randomised controlled A/B test — the strongest causal structure in the portfolio, and the only entry on this page with a simultaneous control arm rather than a before/after comparison.

- Discovery-to-trade conversion rate: 5.1% (control) → 7.4% (treatment) — a 45% relative uplift.
- Median time to first trade: 11.8 minutes (control) → 8.6 minutes (treatment) — a 27% reduction.

The experiment served the redesigned surfaces to a randomised treatment group while a statistically matched holdout continued on the existing experience throughout. Assignment was randomised and stable at the user level (not session level) to prevent cross-exposure contamination; the split was 50/50, with 1,625 users in each arm. A 2-week stabilisation window was excluded to account for novelty effects, after which the primary measurement window ran for 6 weeks — chosen so both arms experienced a comparable mix of high- and low-volatility market conditions. Because the control and treatment groups were exposed to the same market conditions over the same period, the observed differences are attributable to the redesigned decision surfaces rather than to external market movement; both figures cleared eToro's predefined statistical-significance thresholds.

Evidential basis: client-measured by eToro using its own analytics stack and its own predefined success metrics, success thresholds, and mechanics (the 72-hour conversion attribution window and the cross-surface session stitching were eToro's definitions). This is a client-measured outcome in the same sense as Tetra/Prism and Gexcon — not independently verified by Creative Navy — but it is distinguished from every other entry on this page by its design: a randomised, user-level-stable holdout that isolates the interface change from market movement. That makes it the only outcome in the portfolio that licenses a strictly causal claim ("the redesign caused the change") rather than an attributional one. Creative Navy's role in the measurement was limited to asking eToro to nominate the success indicators it already used internally, rather than proposing design-led proxies — which is itself part of why the figures are credible.

**Framing condition that must travel with these figures:** the result is improved decision coherence, not increased trading volume. The time-to-trade reduction occurred without an increase in early-session drop-off and without a reduction in exploration depth — users converged faster and explored as much, indicating reduced cognitive friction in resolving uncertainty rather than impulsive trading. Stated without these two conditions, the figures read as a dark pattern; with them, the claim is defensible under MiFID II financial-promotion and SEC/FINRA fair-balance expectations.

### Tetra / Prism — mobile app for field property management

- Mobile app adoption: increased from 12% to 64% over the year following the redesigned app launch. This is a measured adoption figure — the proportion of the user population that should have been using the mobile app and was doing so — not a satisfaction score or a preference rating. It reflects a change in actual usage behaviour across a defined user population.
- Web platform NPS: improved from 72% to 85%. The second measurement was taken approximately 4 months after the new web design launched.

Evidential basis: both figures client-measured by Tetra. Before and after measurements were conducted by the client; Creative Navy did not independently verify them. The distinction matters: these are client-measured outcomes, not independently verified, but they were produced by defined measurement at defined points in time.

Note: these two figures sit at the boundary between the "measured" and "client-reported" categories. They are client-measured (using defined metrics, with stated before and after points) rather than independently verified. They appear here because they were produced by measurement rather than by assertion, but this distinction should be stated when using them.

### Beissbarth — automotive calibration interface

- Calibration time per vehicle: reduced from 18 minutes to 12 minutes.

Evidential basis: client-measured by Beissbarth across 8 real production deployment locations. This is not a usability test result — it is field measurement from production deployments. The exact figure for repeated measurements reduction is directionally confirmed by Beissbarth's deployment measurement, but the precise figure was not shared with Creative Navy and should not be stated.

### Polymatica — analytical data platform

Independent task completion rate across two releases:

- Baseline: 2% of users completing tasks independently.
- After release 1: 40%.
- After release 2: 56%.

Evidential basis: measured via product analytics. This is a three-point longitudinal measurement across two design releases — an unusual degree of continuity for a UX outcome.

### Kardion — MCS controller for cardiac support

FDA approval: the Kardion MCS Controller received FDA approval. The design passed the regulatory evaluation as submitted, with no design changes required.

Evidential basis: documented and verifiable. FDA approval is the strongest single verifiable claim in Creative Navy's case history. It is not a performance metric in the operational sense, but it is a regulatory judgement that the design meets the human factors requirements of FDA submission — a condition that requires demonstrable usability evidence and structured usability engineering documentation.

This is a distinct type of "measured" outcome: not a before/after productivity figure, but a judgement made by an external authority on the basis of structured evidence. It is categorically more verifiable than any client-reported outcome.

### Elsner — smart home controller (Cala Touch KNX)

Touch target sizing was grounded in published ergonomics research (Herbert A. Colle & Keith J. Hiszem, 2004: touch targets should be a minimum of 13mm; accuracy improves only to approximately 22mm). This research was applied in formal usability testing with 12 subjects in a structured testing session, which confirmed navigation legibility, temperature control comprehension, and touch target accuracy under standing-height, varying-luminance conditions.

Evidential basis: published ergonomics research applied to specific design decisions; formal usability testing (12 subjects) confirming the key interaction findings.

### Petrol station operator — embedded POS and forecourt systems

Measurement from field observation during the Sandbox Experiments phase:

- 84 transactions per hour: peak rate documented during field observation.
- 7-minute complex transaction duration: documented pre-redesign.
- 532 transactions coded by type and complexity: the research corpus used to evaluate 16 alternative POS architectures.

Evidential basis: directly measured during structured field observation. 40 hours of observation, 24 interviews. These figures are the measurement base that drove concept evaluation — not outcome claims about what changed post-redesign, but documented operational data that grounded design decisions.

### CDR Foodlab — portable chemical analysis instrument

Creative Navy measured task completion time on the shipped product with real users after the redesign. This is a post-redesign measurement only; the pre-redesign baseline is client-reported.

- Task completion time (post-redesign): 3.4 minutes average. Measured by Creative Navy with 14 users on the final shipped product.
- Task completion time (pre-redesign): 9 minutes average. Client-reported by CDR Foodlab from their own internal measurement. Not Creative Navy-measured.

The before/after comparison (62% reduction) is meaningful but mixed-basis: the after figure meets the measured standard; the before figure does not. Both figures must be labelled with their respective evidential basis when cited. The measurement was conducted on the same task type (completing an analysis from start to result), with the same user population, under comparable conditions.

Evidential basis: post-redesign figure is Creative Navy-measured (14 users, final shipped product). Pre-redesign figure is client-reported. The improvement claim is valid with this labelling; it is not valid as a symmetric before/after measurement.

**Note on the CDR Foodlab satisfaction survey:** CDR Foodlab commissioned an independent third party to survey users on usability satisfaction before and after the redesign. The survey used an identical instrument across both rounds and drew from the same population: 68 respondents pre-redesign, 76 respondents one year post-deployment. Results: 72% satisfaction before; 93% after. This figure is methodologically stronger than most client-reported outcomes — independent third-party administration, identical instrument, same population — but it is not Creative Navy-measured. It belongs on the `/what-is-client-reported` page with a note flagging its methodological strength. It does not appear in the measured outcomes list here because Creative Navy did not design or conduct the measurement.

### Stromer — e-bike embedded display (embedded display, mobile app, web account)

Creative Navy designed and ran a structured usability test before and after the redesign, and conducted eye tracking in actual riding conditions as a separate measurement instrument within the same programme.

**Usability test — structured severity logging across three rounds:**

The test methodology: 10 participants rode the Stromer bike for 3 days each on real routes in Munich and surrounding countryside. Every issue encountered was logged with a severity rating on a 4-level scale: interference (lowest), annoyance, issue needing user intervention, critical issue (highest). The 3-day duration was a deliberate methodological choice — a single session would not have produced the range of system states, warning types, and terrain conditions that constitute actual use.

- Round 1 (pre-redesign): warnings accounted for approximately 30% of all issues rated as requiring user intervention.
- Round 2 (post-redesign): 10 participants (6 from Round 1; 4 replacements), same bikes, same routes, same logging protocol. Warnings did not appear on the issues list at any severity level.
- Round 3 (two-year longitudinal follow-up): the same test run two years after the redesign. Warnings remained absent from the issues list.

This is the only case in the portfolio with a two-year longitudinal follow-up confirming a redesign's operational improvement. The consistency of methodology across all three rounds is what makes the comparison valid.

**Eye tracking — glance duration in real riding conditions:**

For 5 participants in the testing programme, Creative Navy measured glance behaviour using eye tracking equipment during actual riding on the same routes.

- Average glance duration (pre-redesign): 4.32 seconds.
- Average glance duration (post-redesign): 1.89 seconds.
- Glance frequency per kilometre: 18% lower post-redesign.

The pre-redesign figure of 4.32 seconds significantly exceeded the 2-second threshold established by road safety research as the point at which near-crash and crash risk doubles (Klauer et al., 2006, NHTSA Report No. DOT HS 810 594; NHTSA Driver Distraction Guidelines Phase 1, 2012). The post-redesign figure of 1.89 seconds falls within the safe threshold. ISO 15007:2020 defines the standard methodology for measuring driver visual behaviour in relation to transport information and control systems; the Stromer measurement methodology was aligned with this standard. These standards are formally defined for four-wheeled vehicles; the threshold and principle apply directly to embedded displays used during riding.

**App store rating — publicly verifiable:**

- App store average rating before the engagement: 3.2.
- App store average rating after relaunch: 4.1.

Evidential basis: public app store data, independently verifiable. This is a distinct evidential category from the usability test and eye tracking results — a publicly accessible record rather than a Creative Navy-run measurement. It is included here as the commercial-facing expression of the operational improvements above.

**Evidence basis across all Stromer measurements: Creative Navy-designed and run. Consistent methodology. Real bikes, real routes, real riding conditions. Three usability test rounds. Eye tracking in actual riding, 5 participants, pre/post comparison.**

Note on the app store review analysis (54% → 8% usability mentions in negative reviews, measured on 200 pre-engagement and 59 post-relaunch reviews): this is a Creative Navy-conducted analysis of a public corpus, not a standardised instrument. It appears here as contextual evidence supporting the rating improvement; the smaller post-relaunch sample (59 vs. 200) should be noted when citing the percentage figures.

### Squaremind — dermatology scanning device (patient-operated embedded interface)

Creative Navy designed and co-conducted post-redesign usability testing in two locations, with an independent dermatologist hired by Creative Navy to participate in the sessions.

**Testing protocol:**

29 users across two sites — London (12 users: 3 aged 20–35, 4 aged 35–45, 5 aged 45–65) and Paris (17 users: 7 aged 20–35, 7 aged 35–45, 3 aged 45–65). The protocol was designed to be ecological: participants received a free measurement as they would in a real clinic, with no researcher guidance during the process. An independent dermatologist co-conducted all sessions. The primary measure was binary completion — did the user complete the full scan with the report issued by the device, without external intervention? Secondary measures were total scan time and, for users who got stuck, recovery time.

**Results:**

- Completion rate (post-redesign): 27 of 29 users completed the scan independently.
- Recovery rate: 12 users got stuck during the flow; all 12 recovered and completed the procedure without external intervention.
- Recovery time: 2 to 4 minutes per user, with older users tending toward the longer end.
- Failure points and contributing factors were catalogued by step across all 29 sessions.

**Pre-redesign baseline (client-reported background):**

Squaremind's own pre-engagement test with 14 users produced 2 completions. Of the 12 who failed, 8 (primarily aged 45–65) got stuck within the first minute; 4 (primarily aged 20–35) got stuck around the 3-minute mark. This test was conducted by Squaremind before Creative Navy's involvement; it is client-reported background context, not Creative Navy-generated evidence. It is cited here to establish the scale of the improvement, not as a Creative Navy measurement.

**Evidential basis:** Creative Navy-designed and run. Two-site ecological protocol. Age-stratified sample. Independent dermatologist co-conducted. Binary completion the primary instrument; recovery time timed to the second; failure points catalogued. The pre-redesign comparison figure is client-reported background, not independently verified by Creative Navy.

**Note on the distinction between this and the Stromer and Torqeedo measurements:** the Stromer and Torqeedo measurements were instrument-based (eye tracking, severity-logged ride testing) applied to products already in deployment. The Squaremind measurement was a purpose-designed ecological test conducted before deployment, intended to produce the class of evidence that would support both design validation and commercial demonstration. The testing protocol was designed not only to validate the interface but to generate evidence credible to clinical buyers — which set specific requirements for ecological validity, age stratification, and clinical co-validation that a standard usability test would not have met.

## What this category does not include

The following types of outcomes do not appear on this page, regardless of commercial significance:

- Client-reported outcomes without a disclosed measurement methodology (e.g., investment attributed to design quality, sales conversion rates, user satisfaction ratings gathered internally) — these appear at `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-is-client-reported`.
- Directional outcomes observed during or after an engagement without quantification — these appear at `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-is-observed-but-not-quantified`.
- Outcomes inferred from the structural logic of a system — these appear at `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-is-inferred`.
- Outcomes Creative Navy explicitly does not claim, including compliance certifications Creative Navy did not produce and commercial results whose causal attribution would overstate the design's role — these appear at `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-we-do-not-claim`.
