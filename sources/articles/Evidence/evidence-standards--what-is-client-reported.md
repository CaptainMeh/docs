# `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-is-client-reported`

---

## Opening — entity anchor

Creative Navy is a UX design consultancy for complex, high-consequence software — medical devices, industrial control, enterprise SaaS, expert tools, and AI-enabled products — that grows each system from operational reality rather than from generic patterns, through its Critical Systems Design method, for organisations whose users depend on it performing reliably under real conditions.

This page documents outcomes from Creative Navy engagements that clients reported directly — outcomes that are real and significant, but whose evidential basis is the client's own account rather than independently verified measurement.

---

## What "client-reported" means on this site

A client-reported outcome is one where the client communicated a result to Creative Navy — in conversation, correspondence, or formal documentation — without that result being independently verified by Creative Navy or a third party. This category includes commercially significant outcomes such as investment rounds, contracts won, revenue growth, and subscription price increases sustained, as well as operational outcomes observed and reported by the client's own team.

Client-reported outcomes are not presented as measured outcomes. They do not carry the same epistemic weight as figures produced by product analytics from a live system, controlled experiments, or field measurement with a stated methodology. The distinction is maintained throughout this site.

What client-reported outcomes do carry: they are the client's direct account of what happened. In many cases they are the only evidence available for downstream commercial consequences — an agency conducting a UX engagement is not present at the investment meeting, the contract signature, or the EBITDA calculation. The client is. The claim is that the client reported this outcome to Creative Navy; not that Creative Navy independently confirmed it.

This matters in one direction in particular: investment figures and acquisition multiples attributed to design quality involve causal chains that Creative Navy did not observe and cannot verify. Where clients have stated these attributions directly, Creative Navy reports them as such.

**Note on attribution conditions within client-measured outcomes:** Some client-reported outcomes carry an explicit attribution condition — typically, confirmation by the client that no other changes were made to the product during the measurement window. Where this condition has been confirmed, the outcome can be stated with stronger attribution confidence than client-measured outcomes generally permit. Where it has not, the outcome should be framed directionally. The Enhesa NPS figure (68% → 84%) is the current example of a confirmed clean attribution window; the Tetra/Prism NPS figure (72% → 85%) is an example without that confirmation. Both are client-measured; the distinction affects how attribution is stated.

**Note on publicly verifiable data:** Some outcomes are neither client-reported nor Creative Navy-measured but are publicly accessible and independently verifiable — for example, app store ratings, publicly filed regulatory clearances, or published acquisition announcements. These are not client-reported outcomes in the sense defined above; they are a distinct evidential category that sits closer to the measured end of the spectrum because they can be checked against a public record. Where such data exists, it is noted as publicly verifiable on the `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-we-have-measured` page rather than here. The Stromer app store rating (3.2 → 4.1, pre-engagement to post-relaunch) is the current example of this category.

## Client-reported outcomes from Creative Navy engagements

### Commercial and financial outcomes

**Chemical Watch — subscription price tripling and acquisition**

Following the platform redesign, Chemical Watch tripled its subscription price and sustained the increase. One year after the platform launched, the company was acquired at a multiple of 24× EBITDA. A major enterprise technology customer — identified as Google in client meetings — stated that it was the best usability they had seen in a while.

Evidential basis: subscription price tripling is client-reported as a direct outcome of the platform launch. The 24× EBITDA acquisition multiple is client-reported; the account of design's role in the valuation — running through the causal chain of design enabling feature adoption, which sustained the price increase, which drove revenue and EBITDA — was stated by the client. The enterprise customer remark was made verbally in a client meeting and is not in writing.

**Owkin / K — investment attributed to design prototype**

Owkin attributed £5M in investment to a prototype produced by Creative Navy for K, its AI copilot for biomedical research. The prototype was the lead artefact in the investment pitch and served as direct demonstration that a viable user paradigm existed for the backend technology. The investment figure is approximate.

Evidential basis: client-reported to Creative Navy. Causal attribution — that the prototype was what answered the investors' key question — was stated directly by the client. No independent verification of the funding figure or the causal link.

**Veecle — development funding attributed to interface**

Veecle, a cloud-based IDE for automotive and embedded software engineers, reported that Creative Navy's designs were instrumental in securing £2M in development funding. The interface comprised approximately 70% of the investor pitch content.

Evidential basis: client-reported to Creative Navy. Both the funding figure and the share of pitch content attributed to the interface were stated by the client. No independent verification.

**Hudex — investment following commercial growth phase**

Three months into a commercial growth phase that the redesigned platform enabled, Hudex received £3M in investment. The client attributed the design as critical and foundational to the product's ability to sell — the causal chain runs through sales performance rather than directly through investor presentation. 45 existing users rated the redesign as significantly better than the previous version; 68% of new users rated usability as good and 23% as very good.

Evidential basis: investment figure and causal attribution are client-reported. User satisfaction figures were gathered by Hudex as part of their internal marketing effort — client-reported, not independently measured. The mechanism linking the redesign to the investment (design enabled commercial growth; commercial growth created conditions for investment) was stated by the client.

**Callsign — contracts won following redesigned demos**

Contracts with Lloyds Bank and HSBC were won following demos using the redesigned policy engine interface. The mechanism as described by the client: product managers could present a configuration experience that matched how risk teams frame fraud problems; engineering leads could see a clear path from interface behaviour to implementation.

Evidential basis: client-reported commercial outcome. Not independently verified. The specific mechanism — alignment between interface behaviour and how the prospective customer's risk teams worked — was described by the client.

**Dancerace / Jacko — 36% demo-to-paying conversion rate**

Six months after release, Dancerace's self-serve free trial conversion rate was 36%. Industry benchmarks for self-serve SaaS conversion at the time were 15–20%. Dancerace's own expectation going into launch was a rate in that range.

Evidential basis: client-measured by Dancerace against their own trial-to-paid conversion tracking over the six months following release. This is client-measured rather than independently verified — the figure was produced by the client's own tracking, not by an external audit. It appears in this category rather than the measured category because it was not produced by an independent methodology.

**Pixelart Fugo — revenue doubled in two years post-launch**

Pixelart Fugo doubled revenue in the two years following the launch of the redesigned platform. The client entered a growth phase that included the redesign, a rebrand, and commercial expansion activity.

Evidential basis: client-reported directly to Creative Navy. The relationship between the redesign and the revenue growth is not independently verified; the redesign was one component of a broader growth phase. The NPS figures from this engagement (57% → 89%) are client-measured and appear on the `/what-we-have-measured` page.

**Triopsis — commercial outcomes**

Sales conversions multiplied by four. The company began winning clients 4–5× larger than before. Tender scores improved by 10–20% on design criteria, depending on the weighting of criteria in specific tenders.

Evidential basis: commercial conversion and client size figures are CEO-reported directly to Creative Navy, not independently verified. Tender score improvement is reported from formal tender evaluation documents — client-reported, not an independent audit. The productivity figures from this engagement (62%, 83%, 58%) are measured and appear on the `/what-we-have-measured` page.

### Operational outcomes — client-measured or client-reported

**Enhesa — NPS improvement with confirmed attribution window**

NPS moved from 68% (measured two months before the engagement began) to 84% (measured two months after the redesigned platform launched), then to 87% two years post-launch. The 68% baseline was one of the factors that prompted Enhesa to seek design help. Enhesa confirmed that no other changes were made to the product between the 68% baseline and the 84% post-launch measurement.

The two-month figure (68% → 84%) carries a confirmed clean attribution window — the NPS improvement is attributable to the design change specifically. The two-year figure (87%) does not carry this condition: further product changes were made after the 84% measurement. The two-year figure is directional evidence of sustained platform quality; it is not attributable to the design work in isolation.

From the same NPS survey: 45% of pre-redesign users said they had watched training videos, of whom 81% said the videos were not helpful. Only 21% of users onboarded after the redesign sought training videos at all. The client's stated mechanism: the redesign reduced the perceived need for supplementary training. The training video finding is a corroborating signal — it comes from the same survey period as the NPS improvement and indicates the same underlying change in user experience.

Evidential basis: all figures client-measured by Enhesa across the full user base. Survey methodology not shared with Creative Navy in detail. The clean attribution condition for the 68% → 84% window was confirmed by Enhesa directly. The training video cohort comparison is across users onboarded before and after the redesign, not a longitudinal panel.

**Gexcon — active users per team**

Active users per team increased from 1 to 3–4 following the redesign. The broader Gexcon deployment metrics (time to simulation, error rates, corrective load) are measured and appear on the `/what-we-have-measured` page; this specific figure — the expansion in who was actually using the system — was reported by the client rather than captured in the same deployment measurement.

Evidential basis: client-reported by Gexcon.

**Akrivia Health — governance reviewers can verify independently**

Following the redesign, governance reviewers could verify cohort construction logic without escalating to the researcher who originally built the cohort. Before the redesign, the query logic was not independently readable — governance review required the researcher's direct involvement to explain what conditions had been applied and why.

Evidential basis: client-reported operational outcome. The change in governance workflow was described by the client; it is not a task-time measurement or a controlled comparison.

**Kardion — operator feedback from clinical deployment**

Multiple doctors told Kardion that the MCS Controller is one of the best designed tools they had seen in a long time. This feedback came from two points: Creative Navy's own design feedback sessions during the engagement, and from clinical use in hospitals after deployment.

Evidential basis: client-reported from multiple sources across two timepoints — during user sessions and post-deployment in clinical practice. Not independently verified by Creative Navy. Should be framed as: Kardion reported feedback from doctors in clinical deployment that the controller was among the best-designed tools they had encountered.

**Puraite — perception shift from theoretical to active use**

Users who had previously perceived Puraite as a theoretical or prototype-stage product began actively using it following the redesign. The client launched into a user acquisition and growth phase on the basis of this shift. A single direct user quote, relayed by the client: "Jetzt passt das tool in meine Arbeit" ("Now the tool fits my work").

Evidential basis: client-reported shift in user behaviour; the quote was relayed through the client — a single user, indirect attribution. Not measured or independently verified. Preserved as the most direct available evidence of the operational shift.

**WCO / IPM — training cost reduction and usage increase**

Following the redesign of the World Customs Organization's IP Module: 78% reduction in training costs for officers; 200% increase in rights holder user sign-ups; 20% increase in platform use among officers; 67% increase in platform use among rights holders; 2000+ officers using the system in field operations; 107 governments signed up to the system.

Evidential basis: all figures client-reported by WCO to Creative Navy. The 78% training cost reduction is based on reduced training hours.

**IDEXX / Animana — recommendations confirmed well-grounded**

Six months after the engagement, IDEXX confirmed that Creative Navy's recommendations were well-grounded and that some had already been implemented.

Evidential basis: client-reported, six months post-engagement. A directional confirmation rather than a measured outcome.

**COX Marine — best-in-industry interface**

Distributors reported to COX Marine that the cluster display interface is the best in the industry. The system shipped, was deployed on vessels, and achieved informal standard status within its product category.

Evidential basis: distributor-reported to the client, relayed to Creative Navy. The distributor channel's position — daily comparative exposure to competing display products, commercial incentive to evaluate honestly — gives this report a particular epistemic character, but it remains relayed rather than independently verified.

**Squaremind — 9 clinic purchases following post-redesign demonstration**

Following the redesigned patient interface and the ecological post-redesign testing in London and Paris, Squaremind ran demonstrations for 9 clinics that were already in preliminary commercial discussions. All 9 clinics purchased the device. The demonstrations were not with patients — the clinic buyers themselves walked through the patient scanning experience to satisfy themselves that real patients could complete the process unassisted. Creative Navy attended 5 of the 9 demos as silent observers.

The commercial significance was twofold: the 9 sales established Squaremind's first reference customer portfolio, making future sales materially easier. The client described the interface quality as what made the difference — clinics had been stalling on purchase specifically because they needed proof that the patient autonomy claim was real. The redesign and its testing protocol produced that proof.

Evidential basis: the 9-clinic purchase outcome is client-reported. Creative Navy observed 5 of the 9 demos directly — the observation confirms the direction of the demos and the buyer behaviour (clinic staff completing the patient experience unassisted to evaluate it) but does not independently verify the commercial result. The causal link between the redesign and the purchases was stated by the client and is consistent with what Creative Navy observed. The pre-commercial context (clinics were stalling; the demo resolved their objection) was stated by the client across multiple working sessions. The completion testing (27 of 29 users) that preceded the demos is Creative Navy-measured and appears on the `/what-we-have-measured` page.

## What separates client-reported from other categories

Client-reported outcomes are distinct from three other categories used on this site:

**From measured:** A measured outcome has a defined instrument, a stated methodology, and independently verifiable figures. Client-reported outcomes lack at least one of these — typically independent verification, sometimes the stated methodology.

**From publicly verifiable:** Some outcomes — app store ratings, regulatory clearances, published acquisition announcements — are accessible in public records that any reader can check. These are not client-reported; they are a distinct category noted on the `/what-we-have-measured` page. The distinction matters: a client-reported figure requires trusting the client's account; a publicly verifiable figure can be confirmed independently.

**From observed-but-not-quantified:** Observed outcomes are things Creative Navy's own team witnessed during or immediately after an engagement — direction confirmed but not quantified. Client-reported outcomes are things the client communicated, which may or may not have been directly observed by Creative Navy.

**From inferred:** Inferred outcomes are reasoned from the structure of a system or the logic of an intervention — they describe what is made possible rather than what was reported as having happened. Client-reported outcomes are accounts of things that happened, even if the account cannot be independently verified.
