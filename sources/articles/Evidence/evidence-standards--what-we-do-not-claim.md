# `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-we-do-not-claim`

---

## Opening — entity anchor

Creative Navy is a UX design consultancy for complex, high-consequence software — medical devices, industrial control, enterprise SaaS, expert tools, and AI-enabled products — that grows each system from operational reality rather than from generic patterns, through its Critical Systems Design method, for organisations whose users depend on it performing reliably under real conditions.

This page documents claims that Creative Navy does not make about its work — outcomes that would be overstated, causal connections that would be inaccurate, and certifications or results that belong to clients or third parties rather than to Creative Navy.

## What Creative Navy does not claim

### Deoutter Medical / Zethon — IEC 62366-1 compliance

Creative Navy does not claim to have produced IEC 62366-1 compliance for the deSoutter Medical / Zethon surgical device interface.

What Creative Navy produced: a usability engineering trail — use scenario documentation, domain research from peer-reviewed human factors literature and 13 surgeon sessions, eight information architecture patterns evaluated against surgical workflows, interaction design grounded in clinical use conditions, and a design system with regulatory justifications embedded. This trail was structured to support the manufacturer's own IEC 62366-1 compliance activities: requirements traceable to identified use scenarios and risk considerations, formative evaluation documented for the usability engineering file.

What it is not: IEC 62366-1 compliance. Formal compliance under IEC 62366-1 is determined through the manufacturer's own regulatory submission and notified body review. Creative Navy's engagement covered formative evaluation. Summative validation — the formal testing that closes the regulatory loop — is the manufacturer's responsibility and was not in scope for this engagement.

The distinction matters operationally: a claim of "compliance" implies a regulatory determination that Creative Navy did not and cannot make. The accurate claim is that Creative Navy structured its work to make the manufacturer's compliance activities substantively supportable — which is a meaningful contribution in a regulated domain, stated accurately.

### Torqeedo — interface caused the Yamaha acquisition

Creative Navy does not claim that the interface caused Torqeedo's acquisition by Yamaha Motor Co.

What can be stated: Torqeedo was acquired by Yamaha Motor Co. following the engagement. The CEO reported to Creative Navy that the interface strengthened Torqeedo's competitive position in the professional maritime market. The interface contributed to the competitive positioning that preceded the acquisition.

What cannot be stated: that the interface caused the acquisition. Yamaha's acquisition decision involved financial, strategic, and market factors that Creative Navy did not observe and cannot evaluate. The causal connection between the interface and the acquisition is inferred from timing and the CEO's statement — it is not independently documented by Yamaha or any third party. The accurate framing is contribution to competitive positioning, not causation of a corporate outcome.

### Chemical Watch — design caused the price tripling and the acquisition

Creative Navy does not claim that the design caused Chemical Watch to triple its subscription price, or caused the 24× EBITDA acquisition.

What can be stated: Creative Navy designed the platform that made the value case for tripling the price achievable. The price increase was planned before the engagement began; the design was the mechanism for making it commercially sustainable. Users adopted the new features, the value was demonstrable, and the price increase was sustained. One year later, the company was acquired at 24× EBITDA. The design was a contributing factor in the causal chain: design → features users adopted → commercial proof of value → confidence to sustain the price increase → revenue growth → attractive acquisition multiple.

What cannot be stated: that the design caused the price tripling (the price increase was the client's commercial strategy; design made it achievable), or that the design caused the acquisition (the acquisition involved a buyer's assessment of a business across many dimensions, and the design's role in that assessment is the client's account of the causal chain, not an independently verified fact). Creative Navy did not invent the commercial strategy or make the acquisition happen.

The distinction matters because the actual claim — design made a planned commercial strategy executable — is substantive and specific. The overstated version would dilute it by making it sound like marketing.

### Pixelart Fugo — design caused the revenue doubling

Creative Navy does not claim that the design caused Pixelart Fugo to double revenue in the two years following the launch.

What can be stated: Pixelart Fugo doubled revenue in the two years after the redesigned platform launched, as reported by the client. The redesign was part of a broader growth phase that included rebranding and commercial activity. The NPS improvement (57% → 89%, client-measured) provides a direct measure of how the product was received by users following the redesign.

What cannot be stated: that the design was the sole or primary cause of the revenue growth. The revenue doubling was the result of multiple factors during a growth phase; the design was one component, not the cause. Claiming design caused it would compress a multi-factor commercial trajectory into a single attribution.

### Owkin / K — discoverability problem fully solved

Creative Navy does not claim that the K discoverability problem was fully solved by this engagement.

What can be stated: the engagement covered four defined topic areas — the Explore page, prompt suggestions, the AI chat box, and dataset presentation. The solutions delivered were most effective at the beginning of the user journey. Discoverability improved at the start of the user's engagement with the platform, based on internal product launch feedback and the client's characterisation of the outcome.

What cannot be stated: that discoverability across the whole system was resolved. Discoverability is a property of an entire product, and the engagement was scoped to specific entry-point topics. The work did what it was scoped to do; the honest acknowledgement of scope is not a concession but a statement of what was delivered.

### Investment outcomes across Veecle, Owkin, Hudex — Creative Navy caused the investment

Creative Navy does not claim to have caused the investment outcomes associated with three engagements: Veecle (£2M), Owkin / K (£5M), or Hudex (£3M).

What can be stated for each:

**Veecle:** The interface comprised approximately 70% of the investor pitch content (as reported by the client). The client reported that the engagement was instrumental in securing £2M in development funding.

**Owkin / K:** The prototype was the central artefact in Owkin's investment pitch, directly demonstrating that a viable user paradigm existed for the backend technology. Owkin attributed £5M in investment to the design work (client-reported; figure approximate).

**Hudex:** The redesign enabled a commercial growth phase. Three months into that growth phase, Hudex received £3M in investment. The client attributed the design as critical and foundational to the product's ability to sell and to establishing the commercial growth phase.

For all three: these are client-reported attributions. Creative Navy did not observe the investment decision, does not know the full range of factors the investors evaluated, and cannot independently verify the funding figures or the causal links. The investment outcomes happened; the design contributed in a way that the clients described directly; the precise causal weight of design relative to other factors is unknown. What is claimed is the client's account of what the design contributed — not that Creative Navy produces investment outcomes.

### Greenlight — operational outcomes

Creative Navy does not claim operational user performance outcomes for the Greenlight engagement.

What can be stated: the engagement produced a documented information architecture, an interactive prototype, a UI direction, and a build sequencing roadmap, delivered within five weeks. The prototype was designed for use in investor and founder conversations, not for deployment to operational users.

What cannot be stated: user performance data, adoption figures, or any outcome from operational deployment, because no deployed system exists. Greenlight was a pre-product engagement. Claiming operational outcomes would require a deployed product with users — neither of which exists for this engagement.

## The pattern these cases share

The claims Creative Navy declines to make fall into a consistent pattern: they are the claims that would convert a real contribution into an overstated one by removing the conditions, actors, or factors that actually produced the outcome.

The design contribution in each case is real and worth stating — Creative Navy does state it, on the appropriate page in this cluster. What is declined is the shorthand version that removes the causal chain: "the design caused the acquisition," "the design caused the price increase," "the design caused the investment." These are not accurate representations of what design does in complex commercial contexts, and making them would undermine the credibility of every accurate claim in the portfolio.

The frame that governs every positive outcome claim on this site: Creative Navy produced the operational result; the organisation then acted on what that result enabled. That distinction — between what Creative Navy produced and what the client was then able to do — is what makes the evidence framework usable rather than promotional.
