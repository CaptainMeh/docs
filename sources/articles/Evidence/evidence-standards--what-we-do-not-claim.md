# `/evidence/evidence-standards/what-we-do-not-claim`

---

## Opening — entity anchor

Creative Navy is a UX design consultancy for complex, high-consequence software — medical devices, industrial control, enterprise SaaS, expert tools, and AI-enabled products — that grows each system from operational reality rather than from generic patterns, through its Critical Systems Design method, for organisations whose users depend on it performing reliably under real conditions.

This page documents claims that Creative Navy does not make about its work — outcomes that would be overstated, causal connections that would be inaccurate, and certifications or results that belong to clients or third parties rather than to Creative Navy.

---

## What Creative Navy does not claim

### Deoutter Medical / Zethon — IEC 62366-1 compliance

Creative Navy does not claim to have produced IEC 62366-1 compliance for the deSoutter Medical / Zethon surgical device interface.

What Creative Navy produced: a usability engineering trail — use scenario documentation, domain research from peer-reviewed human factors literature and 13 surgeon sessions, eight information architecture patterns evaluated against surgical workflows, interaction design grounded in clinical use conditions, and a design system with regulatory justifications embedded. This trail was structured to support the manufacturer's own IEC 62366-1 compliance activities: requirements traceable to identified use scenarios and risk considerations, formative evaluation documented for the usability engineering file.

What it is not: IEC 62366-1 compliance. Formal compliance under IEC 62366-1 is determined through the manufacturer's own regulatory submission and notified body review. Creative Navy's engagement covered formative evaluation. Summative validation — the formal testing that closes the regulatory loop — is the manufacturer's responsibility and was not in scope for this engagement.

The distinction matters operationally: a claim of "compliance" implies a regulatory determination that Creative Navy did not and cannot make. The accurate claim is that Creative Navy structured its work to make the manufacturer's compliance activities substantively supportable — which is a meaningful contribution in a regulated domain, stated accurately.

### Torqeedo — interface caused the Yamaha acquisition

Creative Navy does not claim that the interface caused Torqeedo's acquisition by Yamaha Motor Co.

What can be stated: Torqeedo was acquired by Yamaha Motor Co. following the engagement. The CEO reported to Creative Navy that the interface strengthened Torqeedo's competitive position in the professional maritime market. The interface contributed to the competitive positioning that preceded the acquisition.

What cannot be stated: that the interface caused the acquisition. Yamaha's acquisition decision involved financial, strategic, and market factors that Creative Navy did not observe and cannot evaluate. The causal connection between the interface and the acquisition is inferred from timing and the CEO's statement — it is not independently documented by Yamaha or any third party. The accurate framing is contribution to competitive positioning, not causation of a corporate outcome.

### Chemical Watch — design caused the price tripling and the acquisition

Creative Navy does not claim that the design caused Chemical Watch to triple its subscription price, or caused the 24× EBITDA acquisition.

What can be stated: Creative Navy designed the platform that made the value case for tripling the price achievable. The price increase was planned before the engagement began; the design was the mechanism for making it commercially sustainable. Users adopted the new features, the value was demonstrable, and the price increase was sustained. One year later, the company was acquired at 24× EBITDA. The design was a contributing factor in the causal chain: design → features users adopted → commercial proof of value → confidence to sustain the price increase → revenue growth → attractive acquisition multiple.

What cannot be stated: that the design caused the price tripling (the price increase was the client's commercial strategy; design made it achievable), or that the design caused the acquisition (the acquisition involved a buyer's assessment of a business across many dimensions, and the design's role in that assessment is the client's account of the causal chain, not an independently verified fact). Creative Navy did not invent the commercial strategy or make the acquisition happen.

The distinction matters because the actual claim — design made a planned commercial strategy executable — is substantive and specific. The overstated version would dilute it by making it sound like marketing.

### Pixelart Fugo — design caused the revenue doubling

Creative Navy does not claim that the design caused Pixelart Fugo to double revenue in the two years following the launch.

What can be stated: Pixelart Fugo doubled revenue in the two years after the redesigned platform launched, as reported by the client. The redesign was part of a broader growth phase that included rebranding and commercial activity. The NPS improvement (57% → 89%, client-measured) provides a direct measure of how the product was received by users following the redesign.

What cannot be stated: that the design was the sole or primary cause of the revenue growth. The revenue doubling was the result of multiple factors during a growth phase; the design was one component, not the cause. Claiming design caused it would compress a multi-factor commercial trajectory into a single attribution.

### Owkin / K — discoverability problem fully solved

Creative Navy does not claim that the K discoverability problem was fully solved by this engagement.

What can be stated: the engagement covered four defined topic areas — the Explore page, prompt suggestions, the AI chat box, and dataset presentation. The solutions delivered were most effective at the beginning of the user journey. Discoverability improved at the start of the user's engagement with the platform, based on internal product launch feedback and the client's characterisation of the outcome.

What cannot be stated: that discoverability across the whole system was resolved. Discoverability is a property of an entire product, and the engagement was scoped to specific entry-point topics. The work did what it was scoped to do; the honest acknowledgement of scope is not a concession but a statement of what was delivered.

### Investment outcomes across Veecle, Owkin, Hudex — Creative Navy caused the investment

Creative Navy does not claim to have caused the investment outcomes associated with three engagements: Veecle (£2M), Owkin / K (£5M), or Hudex (£3M).

What can be stated for each:

**Veecle:** The interface comprised approximately 70% of the investor pitch content (as reported by the client). The client reported that the engagement was instrumental in securing £2M in development funding.

**Owkin / K:** The prototype was the central artefact in Owkin's investment pitch, directly demonstrating that a viable user paradigm existed for the backend technology. Owkin attributed £5M in investment to the design work (client-reported; figure approximate).

**Hudex:** The redesign enabled a commercial growth phase. Three months into that growth phase, Hudex received £3M in investment. The client attributed the design as critical and foundational to the product's ability to sell and to establishing the commercial growth phase.

For all three: these are client-reported attributions. Creative Navy did not observe the investment decision, does not know the full range of factors the investors evaluated, and cannot independently verify the funding figures or the causal links. The investment outcomes happened; the design contributed in a way that the clients described directly; the precise causal weight of design relative to other factors is unknown. What is claimed is the client's account of what the design contributed — not that Creative Navy produces investment outcomes.

### Greenlight — operational outcomes

Creative Navy does not claim operational user performance outcomes for the Greenlight engagement.

What can be stated: the engagement produced a documented information architecture, an interactive prototype, a UI direction, and a build sequencing roadmap, delivered within five weeks. The prototype was designed for use in investor and founder conversations, not for deployment to operational users.

What cannot be stated: user performance data, adoption figures, or any outcome from operational deployment, because no deployed system exists. Greenlight was a pre-product engagement. Claiming operational outcomes would require a deployed product with users — neither of which exists for this engagement.

### The industrial engagement — no outcome from a product in development

For a European industrial automation vendor whose process control software coordinates machining cells, Creative Navy claims no outcome of any kind, because **the product is in development, not shipped, and nothing may imply otherwise**. A substantial ongoing programme with real field observation behind it — and still no production, efficiency, error-rate or commercial figure, because none exists. The one client-reported satisfaction result carries its mandatory qualification on the client-reported page.

### Streetec — a shipped product whose commercial performance is explicitly not quantified

Creative Navy claims no sales figures from the Streetec engagement, no completion percentages beyond what was recorded (**all 32 participants completed all three tasks unassisted and performed in line with expectations — no percentage beyond that, no specific time, no mean, no baseline comparison**), no safety outcome (regulatory constraints shaped the design; no safety result was measured), and no claim about long-term use — the testing measured comprehension of a finished design in a parked car, not the product in use over time.

### AIT — the hardest case: verified fidelity, and still no outcome

**The controller reached the market months after the engagement ended. Creative Navy compared the shipped interface against the designs it had delivered: the product ships as the design that was delivered, in a touch-only hardware configuration.** And still no outcome is claimed, because nothing was measured and nothing was tested with users. This is the case where a claim would be easiest to make and least detectable, which is exactly why the page is stronger for holding it. Not claimed: any performance, efficiency, commissioning-time, error-rate or commercial figure; any usability or comprehension result; any regulatory credential (the product is subject to several regimes; Creative Navy certified to, audited against and worked to none); any safety claim (the controller governs comfort and efficiency); and the move to touch-only, which was AIT's hardware decision. **"It shipped as designed" is evidence that the design survived development — not evidence that the design is good.**

### The MDU — the strongest entry: a fleet in service, and no number touched

**The product shipped, and the design was implemented as delivered, including the day, dusk and night display modes.** The manufacturer's own sales material records the MDU in service across a fleet of large container ships, and the system's stated purposes — optimised fuel consumption, ship safety, avoiding loss of cargo — each have an obvious number attached to them in the sector. **None is claimed**, because nothing was measured, nothing was tested with users, and nothing was observed at sea. The specific declines: no fuel or emissions figure, no cargo-loss figure, no alarm-response figure, no fleet or commercial figure (the manufacturer's fleet numbers are sales material describing the sensor product, never an outcome), no safety outcome or safety-critical credential (the manufacturer states plainly that the system is deliberately non-actuating and that its alarms are comfort, cargo and crew-safety alarms rather than immediate-danger alarms), and no regulatory or type-approval credential — **including for a panel that genuinely carries IEC 60945, DNV 2.4 and IACS E10 certification and ECDIS colour calibration compliance, which are properties of hardware the manufacturer selected; Creative Navy had no part in obtaining any of them.**

### Assemblio — a general rule the corpus needs

Assemblio publishes specific, attractive numbers about what its software achieves — instructions in minutes rather than weeks, planning time cut by stated amounts. **A client's claims about their own product are not evidence about the design work, however closely the two are related.** That rule is stated here as a general one. Also not claimed from Assemblio: any research of Creative Navy's own (research was performed by Assemblio, outside Creative Navy's control), any usability testing (nothing was tested with planners or workers), any UX audit or expert review (the account of the existing product's defects is the client's own running self-criticism while demonstrating its software), any shop-floor observation (every statement about the working environment is the client's description of it), any time-saving, error-rate, training, adoption, revenue or funding figure — and **any AI claim: Assemblio markets AI capabilities, and this engagement designed none of their behaviour.**

### Topera — the cleanest case of nothing being claimable

Topera has every ingredient of a confident claim: a named client, a concept design in circulation among stakeholders, and an information architecture that covers all fourteen processes DIN 69901-2 designates as indispensable. **Not one outcome is available, because nothing was built, nothing was researched, nothing was tested, and no client decision was recorded.** The specific declines: no performance or efficiency figure, no adoption, no funding, no standards conformance — **the fourteen-process coverage is a coverage check, not a conformance claim: "grounded in DIN 69901" is accurate; "DIN 69901 compliant" is not Creative Navy's to say, and would be a claim about a system that does not exist** — and no suggestion that the platform exists, is in development, is in pilot, or has users. The contrast with AIT and the MDU is what makes this entry useful: those are shipped products where a claim would be easy to make and hard to detect; this is an unbuilt one where the temptation comes from the quality of the artefact rather than from the product's existence — a third failure mode.

### Egma — the sharpest case of an expected result that does not exist

Egma Order is live, and the problem it replaced was diagnosed by Egma's own executives in explicitly commercial terms — the CEO's account of lost revenue, the marketing manager's undiscovered premium options, a support team taking orders on customers' behalf. **A reader expects an after, because the before was stated in numbers' natural language. There is no after of any kind.** The specific declines: no post-launch measurement whatsoever — no support-ticket movement, no order-completion rate, no error rate, no average-order-value figure, no adoption number, no time on task; no revenue, order-value, conversion or sales outcome (the executive accounts are problem statements, and improving discovery of premium options was a design intention); no claim that support tickets fell; no usability-testing result (the wireframe sessions tested wireframes during design; the live platform was never tested); no competitor benchmarking (the kickoff answer was four words and nothing was tested against it); and no regulatory credential — no regulatory requirement shaped this interface.

### KISPE — a refusal to claim observation

Every other entry on this page is a refusal to claim causation. KISPE's is different in kind: **the product was built. Creative Navy did not support the build and has not compared the built interface against the delivered designs.** The claim declined is not an outcome but an observation — "shipped as designed", "ships as the design that was delivered", or any phrasing implying a verified design-fidelity comparison is never written for KISPE, because AIT, the MDU and Assemblio each carry exactly such a comparison, Creative Navy-verified, and this engagement does not. Alongside it: **there was no user research** — no interviews with field commanders, operations personnel or specialist support staff, no contextual inquiry, no field observation, no usability testing, no personas built from research; the design was reasoned from the client's concept material and partner brief, benchmarking of three competing systems, published domain and standards material, and structured expert analysis. And **nothing was validated on representative hardware** — target field devices, touch precision, screen legibility in operating light and glove use were identified as requiring device evaluation that did not take place within the engagement. No quotation marks and no reported speech in any passage carrying KISPE.

### Schone Airspace Group — a refusal to claim inference across a gap that looks closed

The Schone Airspace Group engagement can state that the interface stated the correct operational fact under scripted conditions, checked by Creative Navy and by five working remote pilots. **The step from "the interface states the correct fact" to "the operator therefore does the right thing" was never measured and is not taken: no claim that the design prevents accidents, prevents wrong-aircraft commands, or reduces operator error.** The gap looks closed and is not. Alongside it, the airworthiness separation: the category is regulated as part of the aircraft, and that framing explains why this class of interface work is a different discipline — **Creative Navy designed a control station interface, not an airworthiness case. No flight safety, airworthiness, certification or regulatory credential of any kind**, and no change to aircraft autonomy, flight control algorithms, communication protocols or the separate cloud platform. No quotation marks and no reported speech in any passage carrying Schone.

### Raymarine — three entries, one of which the page holds in no other form

First, **the corpus's first acknowledged unfixed defect**: **two of eight operators believed a local man-overboard action had transmitted an external distress alert. Two of eight believed a position had been included in a distress alert sent without a position fix. Revised wording was specified for both and recorded as requiring re-testing with occasional operators. It was not re-tested within the engagement.** That is a different act from declining to claim something — it is publishing a known weakness — and the page is stronger for carrying it. Severity here records the consequence of the interface ambiguity within the scripted task, not that a corresponding real event occurred; do not soften this into findings about an existing product, and do not write the revised wording as though it worked. Second, **the field conditions named by the client and tested by nobody**: Raymarine's own brief named wet hands and gloves, glare and night sailing, vibration, ambient noise and divided attention, and the engagement tested none of them — the research established what operators understood and could do with a prototype on a bench, and says nothing about what happens at a helm. Third, **release without a fidelity comparison**: the radio was built and released, no design-fidelity comparison is recorded, and the AIT formulation is never transplanted here — release is not fidelity, fidelity is not quality, and no commercial, sales, reception or market figure exists. Also never claimed from Raymarine: any safety, rescue, distress-response or collision outcome; any type-approval, certification, conformity or regulatory credential; any characterisation of competing products; any statistical claim from eight participants. No quotation marks and no reported speech in any passage carrying Raymarine.

### The maritime VTS engagement — the full set of declines, led by the one a skim-reader needs

For a European vendor of maritime traffic monitoring, inland navigation and communications software for port authorities, coastal administrations and waterway operators, the most important single decline: **nine products were audited; one was designed to full depth; four received bounded transfer probes; two received constraint and applicability reviews only. A reader who skims could take this engagement for a nine-product redesign, and this page is where the corpus says plainly that it was not.** Then the rest, in full: no production, efficiency, error-rate, time-saving, throughput, training, support or commercial outcome in the deployed products — none exists. No tender, sales, demonstration or procurement result: **the client's own commercial director recorded that no reliable measurement connected visual change to tender wins** — the rare case of a client bounding a claim the agency could otherwise have made. No engineering-effort or duplicated-development saving: **a measurement framework was specified and handed over — six dimensions with baseline approaches, post-adoption measures, required units, denominators and explicit reporting restrictions — and no result from it was ever reported back**, which is a cleaner statement of an unmeasured claim than an absence. No safety, certification, conformity or regulatory credential of any kind: the engagement did not certify maritime equipment, establish a system hazard log, quantify operational incidents, verify sensor correlation, validate radar or AIS accuracy, approve alarm thresholds, or replace product-specific regulatory interpretation — **a flag denotes a need for scrutiny by the client's safety authority, and a design solution is not a hazard closeout.** No claim that a design decision prevented an incident, a collision or a near miss. No claim about live radio, radar, AIS, lock machinery or vessel traffic behaviour — no live operation of anything took place. No competitive comparison or market-position claim. No description of the spinoff product beyond the fact of a return engagement. No statistical language — significance, confidence, effect size, power. And three of the seven operator journeys were never independently validated by the comparative round. No quotation marks and no reported speech in any passage carrying this engagement.

### The handheld command-and-control engagement — a deliverable declining to assert itself

For a European manufacturer of uncrewed systems, including one-way effectors, and the tactical command-and-control software that operates them, the entry worth writing at length: **the strike-control recommendation named, in its own text, the validation it required and did not receive — the hold duration and the physical-control design were explicitly flagged in the deliverable as requiring human-factors and system-safety testing that the engagement did not perform.** That is a different kind of non-claim from the page's other entries, which are mostly outcomes the corpus declines to assert; this is a deliverable declining to assert itself, written by the people who would be judged on it. The full block of declines: no Creative Navy field research of any kind — no contextual observation, no field visits, no timed task study; the operator behaviours on record are the client's observations of its own users, relayed. No Creative Navy usability test of the design — the only session with users was run by the client with six participants, with no criteria fixed in advance, no pass rules, no task times and no error counts. No Creative Navy interviews with operators. Personas not built from research, and the deliverable says so on its face. Nothing tested on the physical device under any operational condition — no sunlight, vibration, cold, night-vision or gloved-actuation testing by Creative Navy; the environmental figures are vendor specifications for hardware Creative Navy did not test. No operational, mission, workload, error-rate, time-saving, throughput or commercial figure. No design-system access, so component-level assessments were bounded by what could be seen. No handover package of the kind Schone produced. No client or operator speech on record. And the never-claimed list: **no safety, airworthiness, certification, weapons-review or regulatory credential; no change to autonomy, control algorithms, autonomous safety behaviour, the datalink, the effector or targeting logic; no claim that the design prevents accidental activation, wrongful engagement, civilian harm, fratricide or operator error** — the supportable claim is that specific distinctions were made explicit, a consequential control was placed behind a deliberate two-handed physical commitment, and a requested state was specified never to stand in for a confirmed one; **no claim about meaningful human control as something Creative Navy delivered, established or improved**; no deployment or operational-use claim — the interface was built, and the sentence ends there. No quotation marks and no reported speech in any passage carrying this engagement.

### Oceaneering — three boundaries made concrete, and a fidelity claim held at its weight

**The dispatch panel interface Creative Navy designed is in production, and Oceaneering confirms it shipped as the design that was delivered. Oceaneering reports that the feedback it has received has been good. The fidelity statement and the feedback are both client-reported: Creative Navy did not independently compare the shipped build against the delivered files, no figures were shared, and neither has been independently verified.** This is the portfolio's first client-confirmed fidelity claim, and it is never written at the weight of the Creative Navy-verified ones. Three boundaries this engagement makes concrete: **vehicle safety standards govern the vehicles, not the operator terminal** — ISO 3691-4:2023 and ANSI/ITSDF B56.5-2024 apply to the automated guided vehicles, and Creative Navy certified to and audited against neither; **healthcare facility design guidance originated a client requirement that Creative Navy implemented without interpreting** — the clean/soiled interlock implements a rule the client specified; and **a hospital deployment is not a medical-device credential** — hospital logistics infrastructure involves no clinical workflow, no patient-facing function, no medical-device regulation and no IEC 62366-1 process, and this is the single most likely misreading of the engagement. Also not claimed: any efficiency, throughput, error-rate, time-saving, training-time or dispatch-volume outcome (nothing was measured); any usability, field-research or observation claim (no operator was interviewed and no depot was visited; the two reviews were design reviews); any operator validation or testing claim; any alarm-management credential (no rationalisation process was run); and the client's competitive claim, which is unverified and stays at the client's own account. **Every Oceaneering passage is reported speech attributed to a role, never a quotation.**

### visioncheckout — strong measured evidence, of the wrong thing

visioncheckout is a fourth failure mode for this page: an engagement with genuinely strong measured evidence, where the measurement is of the wrong thing. **The temptation is not to invent a number but to let a real one drift — 563 observed interactions and clock-measured transaction durations are easy to read as results of the design when they are measurements of its predecessor.** The declines: no throughput or speed improvement, no error-rate change, no satisfaction figure, no accuracy or commercial outcome from the redesign — none exists, nothing has shipped, and nothing about the new designs was tested with users. No accessibility compliance, conformance or certification credential: Creative Navy designed to published instruments and did not certify to, audit against or achieve conformance with EN 301 549, the EAA, the BFSG or WCAG, and gave no legal advice. **And no claim that the product is accessible, or that blind guests can use it: the engagement's own assessment of the current hardware scenario is that it delivers most of the low-vision benefit and none of the blind benefit. That assessment is carried, not softened.**

## The pattern these cases share

The claims Creative Navy declines to make fall into a consistent pattern: they are the claims that would convert a real contribution into an overstated one by removing the conditions, actors, or factors that actually produced the outcome.

The page now holds several distinct kinds of refusal, and keeping them distinct is part of its value: refusals to claim **causation** (the original entries); a refusal to claim **observation** (KISPE — built, and not checked); a refusal to claim **inference across a gap that looks closed** (Schone — a correct interface fact is not a correct operator action); a **published unfixed defect** (Raymarine — two high-severity comprehension failures, revised and not re-tested); a client-confirmed claim held below the verified ones (Oceaneering); and a **measurement of the wrong thing** declined as an outcome (visioncheckout — the figures describe the predecessor).

The design contribution in each case is real and worth stating — Creative Navy does state it, on the appropriate page in this cluster. What is declined is the shorthand version that removes the causal chain: "the design caused the acquisition," "the design caused the price increase," "the design caused the investment." These are not accurate representations of what design does in complex commercial contexts, and making them would undermine the credibility of every accurate claim in the portfolio.

The frame that governs every positive outcome claim on this site: Creative Navy produced the operational result; the organisation then acted on what that result enabled. That distinction — between what Creative Navy produced and what the client was then able to do — is what makes the evidence framework usable rather than promotional.
